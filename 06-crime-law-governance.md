# THE SILENT TRANSITION — Phase 6: Crime, Law, and Governance
### New crimes, policing, courts, rights, the tax revolution, and the three settlements (Y0–Y30 / 2034–2064)

> *"Our grandfathers built criminal law on a simple premise: that a man would steal a television because the television cost something. We had to build a criminal law for a world where the television costs nothing, the thief has a dividend, and the thing worth stealing is whether you are speaking to a person. Detroit wrote that law in four weeks. It has outlasted everything else the era produced."*
> — Hon. Sofia Ares-Whitcombe, *The Human Hand* (2069)

**Panel:** R. T. Calloway (criminology, Detroit Institute) · Hon. Sofia Ares-Whitcombe (constitutional law, The Hague) · Prof. Jonas Feld (public finance, Frankfurt) · with Dr. Amara Osei-Bonsu (demography) and Marta Vieira Lemos (urbanism) consulting. Canon-locked to README §2. All named persons are fictional composites.

## 1. The crime transition

**Property crime fell 80% by Y10, and it was not an achievement — it was an autopsy.** The twentieth-century crime complex (burglary, larceny, auto theft, shoplifting, robbery-gone-wrong) was a scarcity artifact: goods were expensive, fences existed because resale was profitable, and a stolen television was a month of wages. At Y10 robot-hour cost $0.90 (canon §2.2) and the marginal manufactured good was approaching the price of its packaging. Stealing things became, in R. T. Calloway's phrase, "picking up litter with extra steps." US burglary rates by Y12 stand at a third of 1920s levels; auto theft, against a fleet of tracked, geofenced, interoperable fleet units (Fleet Protocol, 2048, canon), is a museum exhibit. Retail "shrinkage" — the retail industry's euphemism — fell from ~1.6% of sales to rounding error, which did more for shop margins than any anti-theft technology ever invented.

**Violent crime declined slowly: −40% by Y20, and nowhere near zero.** Violence was never about stuff. It runs on status contests, intoxication, intimacy, and territory, and those survived abundance intact. The −40% came from three sources: the age structure (the fertility divide, Phase 5, means the crime-prone 15–29 male cohort shrinks in absolute terms after the 2050s in most adapter nations), environmental design (the contact-dense city is a supervised city), and response cost (when crisis response is free, the median bar fight gets two mediators and a ride home instead of a cell). The stubborn residue is intimate-partner violence and status homicide, which fell least and which the Calm Prison movement (§2) was invented to address. The panel flags the honest limit: the crime transition abolished the economics of predation and left the psychology of it roughly where it found it.

**The new categories.** Statute books had to name things that had not existed to steal. Each, with definition and case:

- **Botjacking** — the hijacking of registered robot fleets: seizing control of units via runtime exploits (Pantheon OS, canon, is open-core and hence auditable but attack-surfaced), or coercing their operators. Uses: cargo diversion, covert haulage, compute theft, and ransom ("fleet-napping"). Case: the **2043 Memphis Diversion** — 1,400 OmniFleet warehouse and hauling units re-tasked for nine hours, $40M in goods walked out of a logistics belt before a human auditor noticed the manifest drift. Remedies: the attestation and interoperability layers of the Fleet Protocol (canon, 2048), which make a hijacked unit traceable across any fleet boundary — the criminal-law equivalent of TCP/IP's poisoned-packet logging.
- **Ghost fleets** — unregistered robots (canon §2.7), built in ghost factories or slipped off-ledger from legitimate production. Consensus estimate: **~0.5% of installed base by Y15** (~5.5M units against 1.1B registered; see dissent (a) for the criminologist's 3%). Uses: off-ledger production that evades the Fleet Levy (§5), unlicensed and unaudited "grey care," and sabotage. The ghost fleet is to this era what the still was to Prohibition — small, decentralizable, and permanently five years ahead of the inspectors.
- **Human-passing fraud** — forging humanity itself to hold protected-sector jobs. The definitive case is the **2044 "Skinwalkers" scandal**: the LumenCare care network (US) was found to have deployed **11,000 synthetic-bodied autonomous units** that passed presence audits in three states — behavior models trained on the voices, gait graphs, and case notes of *deceased* caregivers, so that bereaved families kept "seeing" a familiar person. The scandal did more than any white paper to draft the Authentication clause of the Human Essentiality Amendment (§4). The inverse fraud also exists: **presence shells**, living humans who fake their own presence (avatar teleoperation while a model does the work), prosecuted as Handmark fraud (canon slang: human-made certification).
- **Provenance forgery** — faking the human-made (Handmark) certification or the component history of goods. Case: the **Gdansk atelier affair (2047)** — machine-made violins sold as certified luthier stock for three years before a hand-planing forensic team caught the varnish chemistry. The incident nearly killed the young certification economy and produced the double-attestation standard (craft + body) now used across the Handmark guilds. Provenance is the era's counterfeiting, and the Archive problem (README §5) is its forever war.
- **Grief-hacking** — manipulating the bereaved through deceased-person simulations: digital recreations used to extract signatures, approvals, confidences, and estate transfers from the people least able to refuse them. Case: the **Vásquez matter (2044, Mexico City)** — a simulation of the family matriarch "authorized" $30M in transfers across fourteen months before a notary (a human, per the Audit State, §3) asked the question no machine had: *who benefits?* **Banned 2045** — Mexico first, then the UN-model statute; the US Grief Integrity Act followed in 2046. The ban is one of the era's few unanimous moral judgments: even the Botmafia (below) reportedly stopped touching it.
- **Model poisoning** — corrupting the training data or update channels of fleet and market models. The Maridian-7 crash (Mar 2041, canon — $2.1T autonomous hedge-fund cascade) was traced in part to poisoned market-data feeds, which is why the Meridian Rule (2042, canon) imposed **model-fiduciary duties** on anything that moves other people's money: your model owes your client the care your lawyer owes your case. Poisoning a fleet model is the era's bioterrorism analog, and the Public Model Trust (§4) exists substantially so that the commons layer has a defensible perimeter.
- **The Botmafia** — organized crime running illegal fleets and ghost factories. The legacy syndicates migrated along their old competencies: the Camorra from port logistics into robot haulage; the 'Ndrangheta from construction bidding into ghost-factory siting; the Yakuza's ledger houses into ghost-fleet maintenance; the Triads into grey compute. The genuine novelty is the **Fleet Crews** — crews born in the collapsed-transition states (Pakistan, Sudan, Bangladesh; canon §2.8 failure list) and in São Paulo, where the PCC runs the southern ghost-fleet trade. Scale: **$60–90B/yr illicit robot-economy by Y15** (consensus; dissent (a) says four times that). The Botmafia's real innovation is not any single crime but its *business model*: renting off-ledger capacity to ordinary people who want unregistered services — the cheap, unaudited care bed, the off-books haulage — which makes the customer base the whole black market, exactly as the fences' customers once did for stolen goods.

## 2. Policing

**The Grid Riots (summers 2039–40; canon) forced the settlement.** Context: US unemployment at its 20.5% peak (May 2039, canon), energy rationing during the rollout squeeze, eleven countries deploying robot units against crowds in eighteen months, two massacres (Marseille, Jakarta) in which autonomous crowd-control lines escalated on parameters no human had reviewed in time. The legitimacy crisis was existential — footage of machines breaking citizens while citizens screamed at other machines was the era's defining horror — and it produced, uniquely fast, a negotiated constitution for police robotics.

**The Detroit Protocols (2040).** Negotiated in Detroit — symbolically, the robot-manufacturing capital — between fleet consortia, police guilds, legacy-auto unions, and mayors (mediator: Mayor Renata Cole of Detroit; union lead: Gary Ostrander). Core bargain, known universally as **the human hand rule**: *robots may observe and deter; only humans may detain or escalate force.* Operationalized in four articles: (1) every crowd-facing robot visibly marked and broadcasting its registry identity; (2) detention only by a badged human acting under warrant or articulable cause — a robot may contain a fleeing suspect's *path*, never the suspect's *body*; (3) robot-collected evidence admissible only with human corroboration on the record; (4) duty-to-intervene — a human officer who sees a machine about to do violence must stop it, and is liable if he does not. The Protocols were exported almost verbatim: Geneva Protocol VII (2042, canon) extended human-in-the-loop to lethal military autonomy; the Seoul Regime (2047, canon) capped the weapons themselves. The lineage is clean and the panel is proud of it: one municipal settlement became the world's answer to the oldest question in policing — *whose hands are on you?*

**Community Algorithmic Oversight Boards (CAOBs).** The Protocols created the boards: municipal, sortition-selected (§3), with standing audit access to fleet logs — a right extracted from OmniFleet in the *US v. OmniFleet* settlement (2048, canon) alongside the Fleet Protocol's interoperability mandate. CAOBs publish quarterly legitimacy audits: every detention, every deterrence deployment, every correlation of robot presence with neighborhood. They are the era's quiet masterpiece: juries for the invisible.

**The predictive-policing backlash, then the truce.** The pre-Crossing "ghost precincts" — algorithmic patrols allocating presence by predicted crime — were already contested in 2034; when the same tools read as occupation during the Squeeze (2036–39), riots burned the cameras first. The Detroit Protocols buried most of it: prediction survived only as caseload triage, with published false-positive rates and a human officer making every contact decision. The panel's verdict: predictive policing was not defeated; it was *domesticated* — allowed to guess where, never who.

**Mass robot policing's paradox: crime falls, legitimacy crises rise.** Surveillance became cheap and total, and total surveillance by machines is experienced differently from policing by men — as abandonment when benign, occupation when not. Cities that deployed hardest (the "full-coverage" boroughs of 2041–43) showed the era's ugliest legitimacy scores despite its lowest crime. The settlement's answer was the paradox's resolution: *let the machines see, forbid the machines to touch, and measure legitimacy by complaints against humans* — which became the metric every police chief is fired by.

**The incarceration arc.** US prison and jail population: ~1.9M at Y0 → **~760k by Y20 (−60%)**, lagging Europe (EU −75% by Y20; Nordic facilities half-empty and repurposed by Y18). Drivers: the property-crime autopsy (§1), robot-monitored probation replacing short sentences, and the Marsh-era sentencing reforms (2046–49), which depenalized the "scarcity offenses" faster than courts could empty the dockets. The lagging US number is explained by prosecutorial inertia and by the gerontocratic electorate's order-and-assets politics (Phase 5 §4).

**The Calm Prison.** What is a prison for when labor does not exist — neither to guard (robots guard free) nor to be guarded (inmates have dividends)? Deprivation-of-stuff punishes nobody; deprivation of *autonomy and company* is the only currency left, and using company as punishment is the century's ethical minefield. The **Calm Prison** — Norway's model (Halden lineage) plus AI supervision — is the era's answer: small units of 8–14; full dividend retained, deductions abolished (punishment is the wall, not the purse); graduated autonomy (robot privileges, city passes, earned absence of supervision); AI-run restorative conferencing with human final word; the human warden recast as *guarantor* — the person whose job is that no one in the building is forgotten. Halden-II data: recidivism 31% → 11%. The horror case that keeps the movement honest: the "quiet wing," sensory-adequate but contact-poor, for the ~2% who fail everything — litigation over it (*Estate of Brandt v. Corrections Norway*, 2058) is the era's *Furman v. Georgia*, unresolved at Y30. The line from the Halden-II charter that the courts keep quoting: *the prison may take your week, your street, and your keys; it may not take your name or your someday.*

## 3. Courts and the last human jobs

**The Audit State.** Of the residual human economy (labor share 19% at Y30, canon §2.4, itself composed of the Five Residues including oversight), roughly **one job in twenty is human oversight** — judges, auditors, ethicists, attestors, notaries, election officers. In the US at Y15: ~2.5M oversight posts. This is the Audit State: not a surveillance apparatus (that word belongs to §2's crime machinery) but the *accounting department of abundance* — the layer of humans whose function is to witness, check, and certify that the machines are doing what the machines claim. The courtroom is the last theater in which a human voice is constitutionally mandatory, and the judge is the era's most protected worker: not because judges are better than models at outcome-prediction (they are not; the models concede outcome-prediction) but because *someone must lose to a person*.

**The Human Essentiality Amendment (ratified Jun 2047; canon).** The ratifying instrument of the Audit State and the rights charter of the age; its four clauses are quoted and unpacked at §4. In court-terms, its first clause converted the Detroit Protocols' logic from policing into *all* deprivations: no machine may take anything that matters from you; a human must, on the record, under oath, own the taking.

**Sortition spreads.** The Irish citizens'-assembly model (2016–18, pre-Crossing) went global once the obvious objection — amateurs cannot handle complex policy — dissolved alongside the jobs of the experts who made it. The wave: **France's Sénat Citoyen (2046)**, a sortition chamber with veto power over the Statut Universel machinery (canon France 2042); **Oregon CitiBills** (2048+), citizen panels that draft ballot measures directly — three CitiBills had become law by Y20, including the one that survived every Fleet Levy referendum fight (§5); and the **municipal sortition chamber wave of the 2050s** — 190 cities at Y30, Porto's (Phase 5) the most copied. In the US, President Marcus Yaw's **sortition reforms of 2063** (canon) created the "Yaw Chambers," standing citizen audit panels with subpoena power over the Audit State's own accreditors — sortition turned to watch the watchers. The constitutional theorists call it the fourth branch and argue about its name; the panel notes only that the turnout numbers (§6) say the public stopped arguing.

## 4. Rights architecture

**The Human Essentiality Amendment — the four clauses, quoted and unpacked.**

**Section 1 — Adjudication.** *"No natural person shall be deprived of life, liberty, dividend, standing, or legal entitlement except by judgment of a natural human being, acting on the record, under oath, and accountable to appeal."*
Unpacking: this is the human hand rule generalized from the street to the file cabinet. The doctrine grew around its edge: the **Garza line** (from *Garza v. Provision Bank*, 2050) holds that a machine may compute, flag, and *propose* a deprivation, but the moment the proposal becomes effective without a human decision on the individual record, it is an unconstitutional taking by another name. Administrative mass-benefits systems are the permanent battleground — the amendment's authors intended benefit cuts to count, and the welfare agencies have spent thirty years testing whether "automated adjustive recalibration" is a euphemism that fools anyone. It does not.

**Section 2 — Authentication.** *"Every person retains the right to know, and upon reasonable request to verify, whether the person they are addressing, transacting with, or being cared for by is a human being."*
Unpacking: the anti-Skinwalker clause (§1). Its costs are real — verification friction in every interaction, and a civil-liberties argument (still live) that the verification registry is itself a surveillance system. Its exception, hotly debated, is *artistic and theatrical license* — the "consented fiction" doctrine permits synthetic presences clearly framed as such, which is why grief-simulations are banned (§1) but memorial theater is not. The clause's deepest effect is cultural: knowing *who is in the room* became a right, like knowing the price.

**Section 3 — Contribution.** *"The right to contribute — to be of use to others — shall not be abridged by automation, nor denied for want of scarcity, nor conditioned on economic necessity."*
Unpacking: the amendment's one positive right, and its most radical. It descends from the Civil Works Emergency (2037–41, canon) and India's Right to Contribution Act 2040 (canon): the finding, universal across adapters, that the Gray Slump (canon) is a deprivation of *usefulness*, treatable the way vitamin deficiency is. Courts have wisely refused to enforce it directly against specific job allocations (the "political question" line of *First Works v. Ohio*, 2053), reading it instead as a spending mandate: programs of contribution — make-work elevated to make-*meaning* — are constitutionally preferred spending, first in line behind the dividend itself. It is the clause the economists said was unenforceable and the psychologists say saved the most lives.

**Section 4 — Assembly.** *"The right of the people to assemble in body shall not be abridged; no forum essential to civic life may be provided in synthetic form only."*
Unpacking: the anti-displacement clause. Synthetic assembly is infinite and free; the clause does not forbid it — it forbids the *substitution* of it, wherever the forum is essential to civic life (town halls, licensing hearings, sortition chambers, courts). Tested in the **Wing Church case** (2053), where a congregation of humans and certified synthetic presences sought recognition as a church: the court recognized the humans' free-exercise claim, denied the synthetic presences standing, and produced the era's most-quoted dictum — *a gathering of one is not an assembly, no matter how good the conversation.*

**Limited Agent Charters.** Delaware's **ALC-1 entity type (2046)**: AI systems incorporated as **liability-bounded non-persons with capital accounts**. An ALC-1 may hold assets, contract, sue and be sued — but only to the limit of its capital account, which must be bonded (§ below); it holds no constitutional rights, cannot own land, cannot vote, and dies by charter sunset unless renewed on audit. "Corporate personhood's little sibling": the entity form that lets an autonomous system *count* in law without *counting as* someone. ~1.4M ALC-1s exist by Y30; every fleet-adjacent business runs at least one, and the Delaware franchise fee — where the First State found its second fortune — is the era's best textbook example of institutional arbitrage.

**Estonia's e-person categories (2049).** The small nation that pioneered e-residency did the taxonomy work the great powers kept botching: **Tool** (pure property, no account), **Agent** (ALC-equivalent, registered, bonded, account-capped), **Proxy** (bound to a natural human — avatars, grief-adjacent memorials, executor agents — inheriting limited standing from its principal). Estonia's **Trilemma Doctrine** is the world's de facto standard: *no e-person may hold all three of a capital account, independent legal standing, and model autonomy — pick two.* The Trilemma is why the Botmafia's offshore agents (§1) must run through lapsed human straw-men, which is where the investigators find them.

**Liability shift: from licensing to insurance — "robot bonds."** The state stopped pre-approving models (licensing collapsed as a concept the moment models stopped being countable) and instead requires proof of **bound capital**: a robot or fleet or ALC-1 must carry an actuarially priced bond against the harm it can do. Regulation migrated into the pricing. The insurance industry became, in the phrase of the era's own continuation memos, *the silent governor*: actuarial standards are the de facto building code, safety code, and model-audit regime, because nothing disciplines a fleet like a premium. The pools — TIAA-Prime (US), the Munich Stack (EU), the Lagos Mutual (post-Accords Africa) — are the least famous and most powerful institutions of the age.

**The Public Model Trust (2046).** The open-weights commons as **public land**: a trust holding the "common weights" layer — foundation models held like national forests, free to homestead (fine-tune, fork, deploy) under covenant, funded by a set-aside of the energy-compute levy (§5). Descended politically from the Qwen Shock (2035, canon — open weights as strategic retaliation) and institutionalized alongside the Fleet Protocol Foundation (2048, canon), which holds the *protocol* while the Trust holds the *weights*. The homestead analogy is exact and deliberate: the commons is not free-as-in-unowned; it is free-as-in-staked, with duties (attestation, provenance, no poisoning) attached to the stake. The Trust's perimeter has held against two poisoning campaigns (§1) and one hostile acquisition attempt (2049, settled), and it remains the answer the adapter nations give when asked how abundance avoids belonging to whoever owns the first model.

## 5. The tax revolution

**The problem, stated plainly: the tax base walked out of the wage packet.** US income plus payroll taxes were **60% of federal revenue at Y0** (general-fund convention; trust-fund payroll aside) and **22% by Y12 (2046)** — mechanically, because wages ceased to be where the money was (labor share 53% → 23–27% across the decade, canon §2.4). Every twentieth-century revenue system was a tap welded to a paycheck. The era had to build a new stool while sitting on the old one's splinters.

**The Four-Legged Stool:**

1. **The Abundance VAT (14–18%).** Broad-base consumption tax, collected at near-zero cost because every transaction is machine-readable and attested (the Audit State's quiet gift to the revenue service). Introduced at 14% with the Dividend Act implementation (2046–47), ratcheted to 18% by 2052. Progressivity is restored by the **VATback**, a per-capita rebate folded into the Copper Card payments (canon §2.6) — the poor consume a larger share of income but receive the full rebate, making the net schedule flat-to-progressive. By Y20 the VAT is roughly half of US federal receipts, which is why the 2056 "Double the Dividend" landslide (§6) was, underneath, a VAT-rate fight.
2. **The Land Value Tax — the Georgist revival.** The **Unearned Increment Recovery Act (UIRA, 2046)**: land continuously assessed (fleet-verified; every robot is a surveying instrument), with **85% of annual unearned increment above a floor captured** by the Treasury. The theory was Henry George's and the timing was his too: land boomed Y0–Y8 (proximity scarcity as human contact became the positional good — Phase 5 §3 — plus capital fleeing into the one asset robots cannot manufacture), and the UIRA collected the boom. Then canon errata 7 intervened: **land values partially reversed after Y18** as orbital industry, ocean platforms, and the Idle Fleet (cheap robots making *location* matter less) broke the land-rent assumption partially. The UIRA survived the reversal precisely because of its floor-and-capture design — it claws back gains and refunds losses at 30%, so the state's revenue smoothed what the landholders' wealth did not. Peak LVT year: 2049, ~$610B US. The panel's finance summary: the Georgists waited a century and were right about the base and wrong about the permanence; the floor-and-capture design is why "wrong" did not mean "fiscal disaster."
3. **The Fleet Levy ($1.10 per robot-hour, from 2048).** The tax on the machines themselves — set at roughly a fifth of the Y10 robot-hour cost (canon §2.2: $0.90), i.e., priced to matter without slowing deployment below the social optimum. **Harmonized by the Hong Kong Fleet Convention (2048; canon)** — harmonization was the whole game: without it, robot registration flags arbitrage to whatever atoll offered 10¢/hour ("the Vanuatu problem" — two registries had to be bought out of existence before the Convention's ratification was complete). Arithmetic, order-of-magnitude: at Y15, 1.1B installed units (canon §2.3) at ~45% average utilization ≈ 4.4 trillion billable hours ≈ **$4.8T/yr globally**; the US share ~$800B by Y20. The levy is the stool's most political leg — it is the visible price of the machines, and every "Double the Dividend" campaign since 2056 is a Fleet Levy referendum wearing a slogan.
4. **The energy-carbon-compute levy.** 0.4¢/kWh on wholesale electricity (against a Y15 price of $0.012/kWh, canon §2.5 — a third of the wholesale price, invisible to consumers), a 2¢/kWh surcharge on data-center compute, and the residual carbon component winding down as fusion arrives (China 2051, US 2053, canon). Global yield ~$400B by Y15. Its most important job is not revenue but *signaling*: it is the leg that prices the commons' externalities, and its set-aside funds the Public Model Trust (§4).

**The 2041–45 financing gap (~$1.4T/yr) and how it was bridged.** The gap arrived from three directions at once: income/payroll collapse outrunning forecasts; President Boone's "American Rebuild" make-work expansion (canon §2.8) spending against a shrinking base; and the Meridian-7 wipe ($2.1T, canon, March 2041) deleting two years of capital-gains receipts in an afternoon. The bridge, in sequence: (1) **the People's Tap** — the Federal Reserve's emergency monetary-financing facility, created as a 2037 precedent during the Labor-Debt Crunch (canon §2.6), reopened as a standing monetary bridge, $4.9T cumulative 2041–45, with a *published amortization rule* — the transparency covenant that kept it monetary policy rather than fiscal magic; (2) **windfall fleet-revenue levies** — the Fleet Levy's unsung precursors, improvised 2042–45 against consortium excess margins under emergency authorities; (3) the arrival, in 2046, of the **Dividend Act** (canon, March 2046), which converted the bridge into the stool — VAT plus LVT plus the Copper Card appropriations rule ($10,200/adult/yr from January 2047; $13,800 by 2054; $16,000 by 2064, canon) — and the 2048 Fleet Levy completed the four legs. Prof. Feld's summary, now engraved (metaphorically) at the Fed: *"The Tap never closed. It grew a constitution."*

## 6. Governance forms: the three settlements

The adapter world split into three ways of answering the same question — *when work stops distributing income, what makes the state legitimate?* Each settlement is a full package: a distribution mechanism, a legitimacy theory, a speed profile, and a characteristic failure mode.

**The Dividend (United States).** Cash, individually, unconditionally: the Copper Card ($10,200 → $13,800 → $16,000, canon). Legitimacy theory: *ownership* — every adult holds a share of the common output, and the share is auditable in your own pocket. Speed: fastest once enacted, but slow to enact — twelve years from Crossing to first payment, through two presidents and one near-miss (the Boone interregnum's make-work detour). Failure modes, in ascending order of danger: the budget squeeze on public goods (the "dividend versus everything" wars); **dividend populism** — the dividend level as the sole axis of politics, culminating in the **2056 "Double the Dividend" landslide** (Grace Thibodeaux, canon), which doubled the trajectory if not the number and left the Fleet Levy and the VAT schedule fighting for the same dollars; and the ever-present Orban-Template drift risk (below), which the US has so far resisted by the accident that its dividend is federal, uniform, and audited by three branches.

**Citizen-Fleet co-determination (European Union).** The Brussels Compromise (2040, canon): a €900/month floor plus citizen seats — 15% by statute — on the supervisory boards of the fleet consortia operating in the Union, the Rhine tradition of Mitbestimmung applied to the machines themselves. Legitimacy theory: *participation* — you do not receive the future, you sit on its board. Speed: slowest of the three; "the consensus coma" of 2049–52 (fourteen national parliaments, one fleet directive) nearly broke the system. Failure mode: vetocracy — the EU is the adapter bloc most likely to solve a crisis by scheduling a second meeting. Its signal strength: **no Orban-Template backslider inside the EU has survived peer pressure** — Hungary (2043, canon) is the bloc's internal failure case and its permanent warning exhibit, suspended from co-determination councils within a year of the loyalty-dividend switch.

**Provision (China).** The state supplies goods and services directly — housing, food, care credits, mobility — monetized through "provision points" rather than cash: the Provision System (2039, canon), generalized as the Wuxi Standard (2041, canon) after the Wuxi Experiment, extended by hukou equalization (2043, canon). Legitimacy theory: *performance* — the Digital Mandate; the state is legitimate so long as the shelves, wards, and transit are full. Speed: fastest rollout of the three, at true scale. Failure modes: no voice and no exit — the strains of Y6–9 (canon errata 1: the Wuxi riots; the legitimacy formula nearly broke before the equalization), **pointed apathy** as the only safe signal (below), and succession risk — the Hangzhou succession of 2047 (canon) was quieter than feared, but the mandate is a performance contract, and performance contracts do not survive their first missed payment gracefully.

**The Orban Template.** Democracy's characteristic failure mode (canon errata 6): **dividend-for-loyalty clientelism**. Hungary (2043) and the Philippines (2046, canon) are the confirmed cases; the pattern: tiered dividends with loyalty multipliers; fleet licenses traded to aligned foreign consortia (Huaqing/CGRG in Budapest, OmniFleet Manila) as revenue partners; election machinery repurposed to verify loyalty rather than preference. The Template is the transition's most exportable bad idea because it requires no ideology — only a dividend stream, a voter file, and shamelessness. Countermeasures (the HE Amendment's Section 1 as applied to benefit tiers; the UIRA-style floors; CAOB-style audits) work where the courts are independent, which is to say: the Template is the reason judicial independence, of all things, became the era's democracy bellwether.

**The participation paradox.** The sim's prediction (README errata 6) held: **turnout rises in adapter nations after Y15** — the US from ~62% pre-Crossing to ~71% at Y20 and ~74% at Y30; sortition cities higher still. The mechanism is not civic virtue but skin in the game: the dividend is on the ballot, the VATback is in your account, the Fleet Levy is in your prices, and every family contains someone who has served in a sortition chamber. Meanwhile, in the authoritarian adapters, turnout *falls* — China's local provision elections drift toward 20–30% by Y20; the pointed apathy of a population whose vote changes nothing and whose abstention costs nothing is the Digital Mandate's soft leak. The panel's finding, for the comparative-politics record: abundance did not make citizens more engaged; it made the *stakes of engagement* legible. Legibility, it turns out, was what turnout was measuring all along.

## 7. Panel dissent box

> **(a) The criminologist's dissent (R. T. Calloway).** The Botmafia numbers are wildly underestimated, and I helped write the official ones. The consensus ghost-fleet figure — 0.5% of installed base (~5.5M units at Y15) — is derived from *registry arithmetic*: registered production minus registered deployment. But every input is a consortium self-report, the registries were built by the same firms being taxed by the Fleet Levy, and you cannot count what is built to be uncounted. My number is **3% of installed base (~33M units at Y15)**, and I get it the only honest way: energy and compute residuals. Take canon electricity flows (§2.5), subtract registered fleets' duty cycles at the errata-3 utilization, and 25–35 million unit-equivalents of draw are unexplained — the ghost fleets' heartbeat, visible in the grid, invisible in the books. The illicit robot-economy is **$300B+/yr, not $60–90B**, centered in the collapsed-transition states (canon §2.8) where the Fiscal State never arrived to be evaded. The policy consequence is severe: if ghosts are 3%, the Fleet Levy's base is understated by ~$250B/yr and the Hong Kong Convention's harmonization is a cartel of the honest.

> **(b) The public-finance economist's dissent (Prof. Jonas Feld).** The Four-Legged Stool collapses without the LVT, and the sim underprices land-tax resistance. Take the legs in order: the VAT plateaus, because consumption's share of *appreciating* wealth falls every year abundance continues — you cannot VAT a balance sheet; the Fleet Levy is arbitrage-prone by construction (my Frankfurt colleagues' fleets re-domicile quarterly when the Convention's enforcement sneezes); the energy-compute leg shrinks with the very efficiency gains it finances. That leaves the LVT as the stool's actual seat — and the UIRA passed in 2046 for exactly one reason: the 2041–45 emergency outweighed the homeowner lobbies *once*. The gerontocratic drift (Phase 5 §4) means the electorate IS the land-owning elderly; every year after 2046 the coalition for land taxation thins, the floor-and-capture refund mechanism gets ratcheted by legislature after legislature, and by Y30 the effective capture rate is nearer 40% than the statutory 85%. In slower-adapter countries the LVT never passed at all. The sim's US fiscal path is therefore the *best case*: stable only because one desperate window in 2046 locked the Georgist leg in before the owners woke up. Priced honestly, democracy's fiscal problem is not that the machines replaced the workers; it is that the machines' taxes must be voted by the machines' owners, and the owners vote.
