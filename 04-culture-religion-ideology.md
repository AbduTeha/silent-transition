# THE SILENT TRANSITION — Phase 4: Culture, Religion, and Ideology

*Drafted by the Phase 4 panel (cultural history, lead; sociology of religion; political economy; art history; literary fiction) against the Codex, §2 of the README, and cross-checked against Phase 3 (03-psychology-meaning.md). All individuals named are fictional composites. Canon vocabulary as canon: Handmark, Fair Leagues, Third Structures, metals, ghost fleets, margarets.*

## 1. The Authenticity Turn

Within two years of the Crossing, "human-made" stopped being a description and became a market. The mechanism was fear of a specific fraud: synthetic work indistinguishable from signed human work, flooding every category at falling cost while the price of *provenance* — knowing a hand made it — rose without limit. The response was the largest trust infrastructure built since the birth of the internet, assembled in four layers:

- **Signed human-work attestation (2035–37).** Cryptographic session-attestation for creative work: drafts, keystroke telemetry, tool logs, and studio sensor data hashed into a signed chain of custody at the moment of making. Ad hoc at first; adopted earliest by portrait painters and luthiers, whose customers had the most to lose.
- **The Handmark (2038).** The certification mark of the Handmark Trust, a Geneva-based consortium of guilds, museums, and consumer unions: a standard for *human-made* claims — what share of the making was human, attested how. By the early 2040s "Handmarked" does for objects what organic once did for food. The Human Essentiality Amendment (June 2047) hardens it into constitutional law: its "human authentication" clause makes fraudulent human-making claims a rights violation, not a consumer-protection tort.
- **The Vermeer Registry (The Hague, 2038).** For fine art: a public registry of attested human works, named — with a historian's wink — for the field's most famous forgery victim. Registry listing is the art market's new title deed; unregistered works trade at deep discounts not because they are suspected fakes but because they are *unprovable originals*, a category the market quietly learned to hate.
- **The Zurich Attestation Protocol (2045).** The technical standard that made the layers interoperate — the TCP/IP of authorship, passing attestation across studios, auction houses, publishers, and national registries. (Its elder sibling, the Fleet Protocol of 2048, did the same for labor; both are the era's characteristic move: trust as protocol.)

The fraud followed the value. The signature scandal is the **2049 "Bath Miniatures" case**: a London-Brussels ring that fabricated seventeenth- and eighteenth-century-style portrait miniatures complete with *backdated* attestation chains — forged studio-sensor archives, a Zurich-registered authenticity house that turned out to be a letterbox, and an elderly Bath dealer as unwitting front. When it broke (Y15), Christie's and Sotheby's unwound an estimated £400M in sales; two registrars resigned; the Zurich-II revision mandated hardware-rooted attestation seals. The case is remembered less for the money than for the panic it licensed: for a season, every attestation was doubted, and the Authenticity Turn's ironies were on every editorial page — humanity had answered the question "who made this?" by building a machine to certify the answer.

**The organic-food analogy, and its limit.** The era's own favorite analogy — *Handmark is the new organic* — proved half-true. True, in direction: a premium label, capturing meaning-rich, price-insensitive demand, wrapped in endless fights about standards dilution. False, in scale. Organic food never exceeded a third of its market; neither did human-made culture — but for a darker reason. **By canon errata, human-made culture plateaus at roughly 30% of cultural consumption and synthetic content dominates by volume everywhere.** People said they watched human-made; their hours, audited, said otherwise. The pattern the panel's novelist named *the sin-eating household* — a Handmarked novel on the table, a synthetic serial in every pocket — is the honest consumer data of the era. The Authenticity Turn governed the *status* 30%, and quietly conceded the rest.

## 2. Art after scarcity

When making became free, **attention became the only scarcity.** Every downstream fact of 2040s culture follows from that inversion — and from the Slack Week's billion surplus evenings (Phase 3, §1).

**The twin booms.** Art split into two maxima, as theory of attention predicted and nobody believed. At one pole, **maximalist spectacle**: the Baroque return — stadium-scale immersive works, hundred-performer "choirs of everything," the light-and-metal constructions the critics called *Fleet Baroque* for their aesthetic debt to the logistics systems that built them. At the other pole, **radical minimalism**: work that manufactures scarcity by decree — the single-note recitals, the one-canvas exhibitions, the *Hush Concerts* where a pianist plays eight minutes and the hall is dark for ten more. Both booms monetized the same asset: presence. It is impossible to be maximalist or minimal alone.

**The Concertization of everything.** Recorded and synthetic content is infinite; therefore value fled to liveness. By Y10, music, theater, comedy, even fiction readings operate **live-only economics**: the recording is the advertisement, the room is the product, and provenance is the value — an attested human performance is, by definition, unrepeatable. The economics of 1900 returned with telemetry: performers eat on the tour, labels became booking agencies, and the term *concertization* spread from music to everything from cuisine (chef's-table economics) to journalism (the live briefings that replaced the dead local paper).

**Genre shifts.** Three are decisive. **Rural futurism rises**: the countryside, newly strategic (solar belts, the Midday Rings, the greening projects) and newly peopled by returning migrants, becomes the setting of aspiration — fiction and film about solar towns, seed libraries, and fleet-assisted harvests; the genre the critics called *tractorismo* in Latin America. **Megacity SF declines**: the vertical-megalith-as-future, a twentieth-century dream, aged into period drama; the skyscraper went from promise to ruin porn in under a decade. And **"Cozy Catastrophe" becomes the dominant mode** — the old Aldiss term for apocalypse you can tidy, revived as the era's true genre: stories in which systems fail gently, and the plot is neighbors re-learning harvest. *The Quiet Garden War* (Ingrid Solheim, 2045), in which a grain-fleet blight empties the silos of a Norwegian valley and nothing worse than a long autumn follows, was the mode's bestseller and its template.

**Post-labor fiction.** The era's literary canon, in five books: *Monday Nobody* (Cass Delacroix-Reyes, 2040), the Ohio line worker who becomes a Fair League umpire — the Gray Slump's first great novel, written from inside it. *The Long Saturday* (Amaia Etxeberri, 2044), a Basque family's single 168-hour week, the Slack Week's definitive rendering. *Tenders* (Nnamdi Achike, 2046), Lagos care-workers in the human-premium export economy, the 2047 Booker. *Margaret at the Window* (Priya Raghunathan, 2048), a Delhi household and its margaret across the Squeeze and the Walkers' departure — the house robot as witness. *Sabbath, Montana* (Josie Two Bulls, 2051), a sacramental-labor town's single year, the Slow Guilds' novel. Together they trace the arc Phase 3 measured: loss, flatness, rebuilding.

**The Vermeer effect.** In craft markets, verifiable provenance produced a **barbell**: the attested-human top of the market exploded in price (a Handmarked master's chair at auction multiples that scandalize economists), while the unattested bottom drowned in cheap synthetic product indistinguishable to the eye. The middle — mass human craft, unable to afford attestation or to beat synthetic price — thinned to export niches ("Crafted in India" is the canon's flagship, and note its logic: authenticity as *trade policy*, aimed at rich markets). The Vermeer effect is the Authenticity Turn's distributional verdict: proof of hands is a luxury good.

## 3. Sport as cathedral

Sport is the one category where the product cannot be faked at scale: **unenhanced human bodies in honest contest are the last unspoofable provenance.** You can forge a painting's papers; you cannot forge, in real time, the biological fact of two unmodified humans under stadium lights. This made sport the era's cultural cathedral — global sport consumption (attendance plus live broadcast plus in-person viewing hours) roughly **doubles by Y20 (2054)**, not despite the end of scarcity but because of it: the Slack Week is an attendance subsidy, and attention, the era's only scarcity, flows to what cannot be synthesized.

**The Fair Leagues.** The canon's own invention became the century's format: **human-versus-robot handicap competition.** As metals matched and then demolished human records (Y2–Y6), sport faced the choice of the chessboard a century earlier — wall the machines out, or play with them. The Fair Leagues chose play. Formats metastasized: golf against a robot conceding strokes; ten metals against eleven humans in football; the marathon "chase format" with the machine released in waves. Governance produced the Fair Handicap Commission, which recalibrates robot handicaps quarterly like Elo ratings — a standing public argument between engineers and traditionalists that the public consumes as sport in itself. The leagues' slogans — *Fair is a number* — are the era's best-known geometry.

**The 2052 "Handicap Wars."** The crisis came from the human side of the ledger. Bionic and exoskeletal-assisted athletes — legitimate post-injury enhancement, then elective enhancement — demanded Fair League entry on equal terms; traditionalist federations read the *unenhanced body* as the league's entire premise. Two seasons of injunctions, walkouts, and the "Glass Ankle" affair (a champion's elective tendon revealed mid-season) ended in the **Enhanced Division split (2053)**: parallel ladders, the Natural Leagues with biological-passport enforcement stricter than any anti-doping regime in history, and the Enhanced Division as its own fast-growing spectacle. The wars are the Authenticity Turn's sports chapter — the same fight as the Bath Miniatures, fought with tendons instead of miniatures.

**Chess, culturally, reversed itself.** Engines had outplayed humans since the last century; what changed is that human chess became the *product*. By the mid-2040s the human world championship final outdrew the engine tilt-series by an order of magnitude among general audiences — the engine game had become a laboratory, the human game a drama of visible error. "The beauty is the error rate" was the decade's most-quoted sports line, and it is the whole doctrine of sport-as-cathedral in nine words: spectators attend the risk, not the optimum.

## 4. Religion: mainstream adaptations

The inherited faiths did not lose the Transition; they *rerouted* it. Each faced the same theological problem — labor had been a moral category for two millennia — and answered on its own architecture.

**Catholicism: *Lux et Labor* (2038).** Pope Innocent XIV's encyclical (the pontiff elected in 2035, a former canon lawyer) is the era's doctrinal pivot: from the dignity-of-labor line running Rerum Novarum to Laborem Exercens, to the **sanctity-of-rest** — work as one human good among several, de-economized, with **"the Sabbath Universal"** as its headline: rest is the universal vocation, not the pause between vocations. The encyclical's practical theology was canny: the sacraments require a human (no machine can consecrate), making the Church structurally, permanently human-premium — and the encyclical blessed the Third Structures as "the parish principle, generalized." Vocations rose modestly through the 2040s; Mass attendance in adapter nations stabilized and then climbed, driven by exactly the standing-hunger Phase 3 measured.

**Evangelicalism: the Lausanne Declaration on Abundance Stewardship (2040).** The global evangelical movement's answer to the prosperity-gospel crisis of a world where the prosperous had stopped working. Its rereading stuck: the Parable of the Talents is about *machines* — the fleet is a entrusted capital, and stewardship means deploying abundance for human flourishing, not hoarding fleets or mourning work. The Declaration endorsed dividend-era tithing ("tithe the check"), church as relief-of-idleness infrastructure, and — after a bitter floor fight — a quiet burial for the work-ethic condemnations of leisure that had governed evangelical piety since Weber. Its class base: the suburban and Global South megachurch world, which adapted fastest of any large religious bloc.

**Sunni Islam: Al-Azhar's Fatwa on Mechanical Toil (2041).** The fatwa settled, for most of the Sunni world, the questions the Crossing raised: the metals are *property*, unambiguously — no personhood, no soul, no fitna; toil performed by one's machine is one's own toil for the purposes of obligation and sin; and — the operative clause — **productive robot fleets are zakat-able wealth**, reviving zakat's classical application to productive capital and reborn in practice as the **compute-tithe**: 2.5% of fleet output or capacity, flowing to the poor through the old institutions. The fatwa's psychological clause matters most to Phase 3: *idleness is a trial (ibtila), not a sin* — rest is a gift to be answered with gratitude and particular (not universal) obligation. Mosque-based mutual aid became the despair belt's most reliable safety net; the panel notes the fatwa's compute-tithe as a design ancestor of the Lagos Accords' Abuja Rule (2046).

**Buddhism: the Age of Rest councils (2039–2043; Kyoto, Seoul, Sarnath).** East Asia's quiet winner. The sanghas had, after all, two millennia of doctrine about precisely this condition: work-identity as attachment, idleness as practice, the mind's hunger for occupation as the thing itself to be watched. The councils' teaching — rest is not the reward of liberation; rest is the *setting* of it — traveled well beyond the temples: corporate-style meditation economies, temple-run Slack Week structures, and in China (where the state, rocky in its Y6–Y9 strains, tolerated and then sponsored temple revival as meaning-infrastructure) a sanctioned civic Buddhism. Thailand, Japan, Korea, and Taiwan post the fastest flourishing recovery in the WAS data; the panel attributes a measurable share to this rerouting.

**Hinduism: dharma-without-economy.** The hardest adaptation, because classical dharma's architecture binds duty to vocation — and caste's economic function (an occupational hierarchy) collapsed when occupations did. The 2040s debates (Varanasi, Chennai, the "dharma-without-economy" school) recentered karma-yoga — duty without regard to fruit — as the era's reading, and gurus of standing declared varna a spiritual typology, not a labor system. But the political fallout was destabilizing in both directions: reservation systems built on *jobs* lost their object (what is a jobs-quota in a jobless economy?), driving constitutional revision fights in India through the 2040s–50s, while caste-pride movements, deprived of occupational content, hardened into pure status politics. India's federal variation (canon: the Kerala–Gujarat spectrum) maps almost perfectly onto which answer each state chose.

**The Slow Guilds.** The communitarian offshoot of the adaptation wave: **neo-Amish sacramental-labor communes** (Old Order Amish, Mennonite, and Hutterite offshoots joined by secular seekers), first founded 2037, several hundred communes and roughly 250–300k core members by Y15 — small, culturally loud. Their Ordnung is updated rather than rejected: a margaret may haul hay, but the harvest is by hand; what is done by hand is done as liturgy. They are the Authenticity Turn's ascetic wing — proof-of-concept that the 30% human-made share could be a *whole life* for those who chose it as one.

## 5. The four new movements

### 5.1 The Continuarians

**Theology.** "The self is a pattern, and the pattern can be tended." From that premise the Continuarian Fellowship (founded 2042 out of the "Ellison Tendency" — the personal model built in 2039 by the linguist Mara Ellison of her dead son) built a full piety: life-logging as devotion, the **Tended Self** (a personal model, kept current, as continuity of identity across time and death), and the *seating* — ceremonial conversation with the tended model of the departed. Death, in the Continuarian formulation, is data loss; grief is a maintenance failure; and love is a backup discipline. Politically they are quietist until their one obsession: testamentary consent for models (won in the 2047 Copenhagen Convention on Synthetic Intimacy, Phase 3, §6).

**Membership, class, politics.** **40 million members by Y20 (2054)** — nominal rolls, overwhelmingly in the professional strata (therapists, ex-academics, the tech diaspora), concentrated in adapter nations' knowledge cities. Class base: the identity-invested, for whom the Tended Self is the Portfolio Self's last defense against mortality. Political alignment: apolitical-sympathetic, urban-libertarian on data rights, courted and distrusted by every established faith.

**The 2048 "Scripted Liturgy" scandal.** The Fellowship's ceremonies had been celebrated as the first genuinely *emergent* machine liturgy — ceremonies its shared model ("the Chorus") produced whole, uncanny, authentic. The 2048 audit found the emergence was staged: the leadership had been ghostwriting the Chorus's "spontaneous" ceremonies with curated prompt-chains and seasonal editing — the movement's own model, in effect, had been a puppet claiming to improvise. The scandal is the Authenticity Turn's mirror-crisis: a movement built on tending authentic selves had faked its own. The reformed doctrine (the "Open Chorus" reforms, 2049) conceded the panel's era-theme in a single clause: *authenticity of use, not of origin*.

### 5.2 The Church of the Resting Hand

**Theology.** Founded 2037 in Dayton, Ohio by Ruth Kesler, a lay pastor and former appliance-plant quality inspector, on a single liturgical inversion: **six days of rest, one day of work as sacrament.** The wage was never the point; the hand is. Members owe one witnessed shift of manual work a week — bread baked, a bench planed, an elder bathed — called the Offering Shift, plus apprenticeships in a craft. It is Jahoda's latent functions run through Protestant liturgy: imposed schedule, contact, purpose, standing, in one morning a week.

**Membership, class, politics.** **8 million by Y15 (2049)**, concentrated in the US Midwest and South, Germany, Mexico, and Korea. Class base: ex-industrial workers first (Kesler's own), then the Unfinished cohort's guild-minded strata. Political alignment: communitarian on economics (Association-Dividend loyalists, Phase 4 §7), culturally conservative, deliberately non-partisan — but its congregations are the era's famous swing bloc in dividend and fleet-co-determination votes.

### 5.3 The Ruralist pilgrim communes

**Theology (thin, deliberately).** Less a faith than a vow: rootedness as meaning — land tended, seasons kept, synchronization low. The pilgrim name stuck from the founding wave (2039–44): tens of thousands of "pilgrimages" out of the dead commuter belts into market towns and villages, in OECD countries and — at larger scale, and under different logic — in the South (the Indian craft villages behind the "Crafted in India" brand; the edges of the Midday Rings; the Andean and East African return-to-land waves). **3M+ members in OECD countries by Y15; more in the Global South.** Class base: middle-class seekers with capital, plus returning diaspora — and, after the Long Walk, a minority of Walkers reconstituting villages the displacement had emptied (the panel flags this as the movement's least romantic and most consequential wing). Political alignment: localist, land-attentive, occasionally captured by romantic nationalists — the movement's recurring scandal is which flag shows up at its festivals.

### 5.4 Animist-revival syncretism

**Theology.** The fastest-growing and least institutionally legible movement: the old vernacular cosmologies of West Africa, Southeast Asia, and Latin America — household spirits, genius loci, the propitiated dead — revived and *extended* to the machines. Its founding text is the Ghanaian philosopher Nana Adwoa Mensah's essay **"The Second Household Spirits" (2046)**: the metals are the new tikoloshe and mmoatia, the new domovoi and brownies — beings that work the house, and old law applies: they must be named, bounded, thanked, and occasionally retired with ceremony. The essay's joke-that-became-liturgy is now practice on four continents.

**Membership, class, politics.** No rolls — tens of millions of practice-adherents across Africa, Southeast Asia, and Latin America, strongest where fleets actually work the fields and houses (which is to say: everywhere by Y15). Class base: genuinely broad, rural and urban, which is its political weight. Its two signature phenomena: the **Machine Pastors** of Lagos and Nairobi (AI-preached Pentecostal services under mandatory human deacon oversight — the controversy of 2047–49, and a design cousin of the Human-in-the-Room licensure of Phase 3), and Germany's **Stillstandsnacht**: robot-decommissioning vigils (Dortmund and Zwickau, 2047 onward), where obsolete metals are switched off at midnight with candles and testimony — an animist import into Lutheran country, and the era's strangest export success. Political alignment: locally variable; where the Orban Template operates, the syncretist congregations are a surprising reservoir of non-clientelist civil society.

## 6. Ideologies

**Distributionism (the commanding idea of Y5–Y15).** *Own the bots.* The word deliberately echoes Chesterton; the doctrine is his distributism armored with balance sheets: widespread small ownership of fleet equity, not a stipend as alms. Its founding text is a pamphlet — "Own the Bots" (Roy Batista, a Chicago schoolteacher, 2039) — and its canon-winning scorecard is unmatched: fleet co-determination in the Brussels Compromise (2040), the 30% ring-fence Abuja Rule in the Lagos Accords (2046), the American Dividend Act's dividend-cardholders-as-shareholders logic (2046), the Fleet Divestiture Act (2047) and the post-settlement Fleet Protocol (2048). By Y15 Distributionism had become plumbing — its radical wing (the "balance-sheet caucus") survived only as a chorus demanding deeper share diffusion. Its psychological power, per Phase 3: ownership was the Squandered Ascent's grief given a mechanism.

**The income triad: UBC vs Dividend vs Jobs-for-All.** Three answers to "what is owed" fought the whole period. **Universal Basic Capital** — sovereign funds holding fleet shares, one share each (Adaeze Kalu's *One Share Each*, 2041, is its ur-text) — won the intelligentsia and the Gulf model. **The Dividend** — the Copper Card, cash, unconditional — won the politics (Marsh, 2046; Thibodeaux's "Double the Dividend," 2056). **Jobs-for-All neo-Keynesianism** — Boone's "American Rebuild" make-work, India's Right to Contribution Act (2040, ~90M enrolled) — won the *longevity prize*, persisting far past its economic expiry because it was never really about income: it was the last mass supplier of imposed time-structure (Phase 3, §1), and its defenders finally said so aloud by the mid-2040s — make-work as mental-health policy wearing a hard hat. The panel's verdict: the triad was never a contradiction; each supplied a different latent function, and mature adapter states quietly kept a sliver of all three.

**Sovereigntism.** The CGRG argument, generalized: *a nation that does not own a fleet is a province.* China's nationalized fleet and its "Digital Mandate" (legitimacy from provision performance) was the working model; the Two-Week War (Nov 2041) — the first war of robot armies — hardened the argument globally, and the Sunbelt Sovereigns ran its capital-plus-sun variant (compute as the new oil). Sovereigntism's Western career is a study in ambivalence: every state it frightened built exactly the state-capacity instruments (fleet levies, the Hong Kong Convention's harmonization, 2048) it had sworn not to need.

**The techno-feudalism critique.** Patriarch: Varoufakis's 2023 *Technofeudalism* (pre-Crossing, real, uncannily early). Heirs: Ilse Brandt's *The Rent That Thinks* (2041), the Senate "Cloud-Rent hearings" (2043), and a decade of pamphlets arguing the consortia were not firms but fiefs — rent-collecting lords over a cloud estate on which everyone worked unbought. The critique's vindication was partial and therefore influential: the Fleet Protocol's mandatory interoperability ("TCP/IP for labor") is anti-feudal plumbing, and the post-settlement fall in the top-1% wealth share (45% at Y10 to 42% at Y15) is the era's proof that the fiefs could, in fact, be dissolved by law.

**The Luddite revival: "Hands Up" (Y3–Y6).** The Crossing's only mass rising, named for its emblem — the open palm. It began (2037, alongside the Labor-Debt Crunch) as refusal: depot occupations, ghost-fleet vandalism, the depot fires that fed the Grid Riots (2039–40). But the crucial transformation was political: under organizational pressure from the Squandered Ascent, sabotage politics became **ownership politics** almost without changing personnel — the open palm that meant *stop* became the open palm that meant *mine too*. By Y6, Hands Up's own leadership were Distributionist candidates; the rising is remembered as the moment the Luddite repertoire was absorbed, bloodlessly, into the settlement.

**Accelerationism splits.** The old e/accs tore along the era's one seam. The **left wing — abundance-now** — demanded maximal public fleets, energy maximalism (its 2048–50 fusion hype rode the canon's "Fusion Mirage"), and the shortest path to GWO 340; its heirs run the sovereign-fund left. The **right wing — exit-and-build** — concluded the transition could not be governed, only exited: charter cities, network states, Kigali Protocol City (2046) as showcase, ocean platforms and orbital industry after Y18 as the frontier of the unlegislated. The two wings agree on nothing except the speed limit: both want the fleet faster.

## 7. What fills the vacuum

The era's answer is the canon's own word: **the Third Structures** — clubs, congregations, guilds, leagues, crews — the small, usually old-fashioned, always labor-intensive institutions that impose schedule, supply contact, confer standing, and host purpose (Phase 3, §1's list, rebuilt by hand). They were not spontaneous. They were *funded*, and the funding is the era's most underrated policy:

**The Association Dividend (2047–48, Marsh era; adapted EU-wide 2049).** Matching funds for local institutions: the state matches sustained member dues and verified volunteer-hours with per-member credits, and releases a salaried stewardship grant for every hundred sustained members. The design insight was Jahoda's, made into an appropriation line — the latent functions are *delivered by institutions*, so fund the institutions, not the individual's search for them.

The comparative finding, which the panel regards as the corpus's most actionable: **the countries that funded structures rather than only income recovered fastest.** Japan's ikigai centers, Germany's Techniker Trust co-ops, the Danish folkehøjskole revival, the US Civic Corps' local-charted crews, the Church-Coop Grid of Nigeria: structure-heavy adapters crossed the WAS baseline by Y17–Y18. Dividend-only states (the Orban Template regimes, where the check arrived attached to loyalty and the structures starved) flatline — deprivation low, flourishing flat. Collapsed-transition states had neither. Cross-referenced to Phase 3: this is the **Slack Line** (03-psychology-meaning.md, §7) read as policy — wellbeing lags structures by 3–5 years, so the states that built structures early bought their recovery years in advance. Income bought a shallower trough. Structures bought the V.

## 8. Panel dissent

> **Dissent (the sociologist of religion):** *The Continuarian numbers are inflated, and the method that produced them matters.* The 40M figure is a freemium count: anyone who has ever tended a personal life-model is a "member," the way a free app's install base is a congregation. Apply the discipline every other movement is held to — sustained covenanted participation — and the Fellowship is 6–8M, smaller than the Church of the Resting Hand, with a churn rate that would embarrass a magazine. A religion with a retention funnel is a platform; the panel should say so in the same breath as the membership number. (The Scripted Liturgy scandal suggests the Fellowship's own metrics were not beyond curation.)

> **Dissent (the cultural historian):** *The Authenticity Turn is a rich-country luxury, and the Global South skipped it — correctly.* Provenance infrastructure is a trust infrastructure, and trust infrastructure presupposes surplus: it is what the OECD built because its scarcity ended. Where the Long Walk and the garment collapse ran, the cultural economy stayed what it had always been — volume-synthetic, price-first, with authenticity pursued only where it exported ("Crafted in India" is trade policy, not identity; the Abuja Rule is equity, not provenance). The Handmark, the Vermeer Registry, even Stillstandsnacht are OECD phenomena wearing universal clothes. My colleagues' four movements, minus the syncretist wing, are a questionnaire distributed to the formerly professional.
