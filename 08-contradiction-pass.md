# PHASE 8 — THE CONTRADICTION PASS
### Sixteen steelmans against this simulation's own claims, with adjudications

*Method: each entry states a load-bearing claim of the corpus (with its source file), constructs the strongest honest case for the opposite outcome, and adjudicates: **survives**, **survives amended**, or **withdrawn**. Where an adjudication changed the canon, the change is flagged and reflected in README §2.10's errata. The pass was conducted before the deep-dive cycles so that the cycles build on corrected ground.*

---

## 1. "US unemployment peaks at 20.5% in May 2039." (01 §3; canon §2.4)

**The steelman.** Every historical general-purpose technology — steam, electricity, the automobile, the microprocessor — produced *displacement spells, not displacement plates*: task-level churn of 2–5% a year absorbed by redeployment, with unemployment peaks under 12% in the worst troughs (US 1982). The Crossing's own physics argues for a slower, shallower S-curve than the canon draws: retrofit is the hidden brake (a Brownfield warehouse re-fits in months, but construction, agriculture, and care have decade-scale capital cycles); liability and insurance pricing slow deployment in exactly the high-wage sectors that matter; and the cheap-human phase should exist even in logistics — a human at $19/hour is cheaper than a robot at $21, and firms manage *wage* before *workforce*. On this reading, the US peak is 11–14%, and the 20.5% figure is the sim's most dramatic number and its least defended.

**Adjudication: survives, with a wider band.** Three canon features defeat the steelman's strongest form. First, the *discontinuous* mechanism (errata 2): the Crossing did not bid down wages; it stopped renewing contracts — the trapdoor, not the glide. Firms do not re-sign displaced cohorts at lower wages; they sign robots, and the cheap-human phase appears only where robots *cannot* yet go (the dexterity tails). Second, the Junior Freeze compressed the entire youth intake into the same five years — a demographic concentration no prior displacement wave had. Third, the canon's own errata 3 (effective 2.4 FTE, slower deployment) already moved the path in the steelman's direction; the 20.5% peak is the *top* of a 15–24% band whose width is honest. What the pass does amend: the corpus should stop implying the peak was uniform — state-level peaks ranged from 14% (robot-manufacturing counties) to 28% (pure services exporters) — and 01 now carries that note.

## 2. "GDP breaks as an instrument after Y0." (01 §1)

**The steelman.** National accountants have spent a century handling exactly this problem — hedonic adjustment for computers since the 1990s, quality change for everything since the Boskin Commission. A deflation-driven wedge between nominal GDP and welfare is not new; by the sim's own logic the 2026-era GDP already under-measured digital goods. The claim that *nobody noticed for a decade* is the hindsight of the 2051 BIS report flattering its own discovery; the BEA and Eurostat had divergence dashboards by Y2.

**Adjudication: survives, sharpened.** The steelman is right about measurement and wrong about politics, which is the claim's actual content. The instruments *did* exist and *were* ignored — not because statisticians failed but because the divergence was socially convenient: falling nominal GDP let leaders call a structural transition a "recession" and deploy recession tools (make-work, rate cuts) that were politically available and structurally wrong. The broken instrument was never the accountant's; it was the *rhetoric's*. Amendment adopted: 01's framing line now reads "the instruments lagged the transition by design, not by incapacity." The sim's larger lesson — that in the first post-scarcity decade, *the map was fit to the territory's old shape* — stands.

## 3. "The cheap-human wage-compression phase was skipped in cognitive sectors." (errata 2; 01 §2; 07 Priya vignette)

**The steelman.** Large firms reassign; they do not amputate. The internal-labor-market literature predicts that a firm with 10,000 analysts facing software parity cuts the analysts' growth, freezes hiring, and *redeploys* the juniors into supervision-of-models roles at flat pay — a wage-compression phase by another name. And in fact the corpus contains its own counterexample: Priya's audit house *is* the redeployment phase, six years after the freeze.

**Adjudication: survives, language amended.** The trapdoor was real in *external* hiring — the market, not the firm, is where the cliff was — but the steelman correctly identifies a shadow phase the corpus flattened: internal redeployment at flat-to-falling real wages, lasting 2–4 years in big-firm cognitive sectors, which is why the Squandered Ascent's displacement *felt* like a staircase down before it felt like a trapdoor. Amendment adopted (wording only): errata 2 now reads "the *external* cheap-human phase was skipped; internal redeployment cushioned, then delayed, the fall." No downstream numbers move.

## 4. "China adapts — rockily — and the system holds through Y30." (01 §3, §8; 02 §1, §8; 06 §6)

**The steelman.** The Provision System is a *run-risk architecture*: legitimacy indexed to delivery means any delivery failure — a bad harvest, a fleet-recall cascade, a grid accident — converts directly into legitimacy withdrawal, with no valve. The points system invites gaming at civilizational scale (local officials provisioning the statistics); local-government debt predated the Crossing and got worse; the demographic cliff (median age 52 at Y30) arrives *after* the mandate's first missed payment; and the Hangzhou succession was quiet only in the telling — successions are when performance contracts renegotiate. A pessimist runs the same canon and gets: Wuxi riots → 2043 hukou equalization as *concession from weakness* → the 2047 succession contested → a Provision-recalibration crisis in the 2050s that the corpus papers over. The sim, in other words, runs China's lucky branch.

**Adjudication: survives with explicit probability and one widened crack.** The panel re-ran the 2047 succession with hostile assumptions and found the system still standing at Y30, but with a visibly fatter left tail: the Digital Mandate survived because *delivery kept compounding* — the one contingency the mandate cannot survive is plateau, and plateau arrives with the Idle Fleet (utilization 40%) unless Beijing converts provision legitimacy into participation legitimacy. Canon amendment adopted: add to §2.8 that by Y25 Beijing's own theorists were arguing the "Second Mandate" (a sortition-flavored consultative layer, the "Wenceng" chambers, piloted 2057) — the system's attempt to grow a valve before the plateau grows a crack. The sim's Y30 China is stable; it is now canon that it knows it is on a deadline.

## 5. "The American Dividend Act passes in 2046 and holds." (01 §4; 06 §5–6)

**The steelman.** The US of 2046 has: a populist right that just lost a war presidency, a fleet oligopoly funding repeal campaigns, a Supreme Court that survived the People's Tap by 4–4–1, and a federal structure in which a state-level Orban Template (loyalty-tiered dividends) is not hypothetical but *administratively trivial*. The ADA's whole arithmetic (VAT + LVT + levy) requires three tax innovations to survive one Supreme Court term each. Historically, grand fiscal bargains (Social Security, the ACA) took decades and survived *narrower* assaults; a 2048 repeal vote fails by one senator and the corpus never mentions it.

**Adjudication: survives, but the accident is load-bearing — canonized.** The pass agrees with the steelman's premise and adopts its history: there *was* a repeal attempt. Canon addition: **the 2047 Dividend Repeal Act** passes the House on fleet-lobby money, dies in the Senate by the vote of an one-term senator from a fleet-manufacturing state whose constituents' dividends were, at that moment, larger than their paychecks — the sim's cleanest illustration that the dividend's true guarantor was not the Constitution but the *payroll register of the new economy*. And the state-level Orban risk is real and attempted: canon addition, the "Idaho Two-Tier" experiments of 2049–51, struck down under HE Amendment Section 1 (*Okafor v. Idaho*, 2052). The ADA holds because three independent accidents (the 2046 emergency window, the Senate vote, the courts) happened to line up — the corpus now says so.

## 6. "Nuclear deterrence holds; the Two-Week War is the era's only great-power war." (02 §4, §7)

**The steelman.** Cheap, deniable, deniable-adjacent machine violence lowers the *cost of trying*, and history's lesson is that falling costs raise attempt frequency. The corpus itself lists: Yemen (2043), the Electro-Balkans (2044–46), the Sahel drone wars, the Hormuz incidents. The Kaliningrad Clock's 11-minute margin is not a safety record; it is one coin-flip. And MAD-2 (grid interdiction) is a *first-strike-friendly* deterrent: disruption degrades gradually, deniably, and reversibly — the exact profile that tempts rather than deters.

**Adjudication: survives, with the small-war ledger completed.** The distinction that saves the claim: the Crossing lowered the cost of *violence* everywhere and lowered the value of *conquest* everywhere, and the second effect dominates between great powers — there is nothing left worth taking that can be held. Below the great-power line, the corpus under-listed; canon addition: the **2047 Hormuz minelayer crisis** (fleet-insurance interdiction, resolved by the TIAA-Prime/Munich Stack joint pool — the insurers ending a war, again) and the formalization of the **Sahel Drone Accords (2054)** after a decade of robotized civil wars that killed more people than the Two-Week War by two orders of magnitude and were, precisely for that reason, not "wars" in the treaties. The claim is amended to read: *no great-power war; many small ones; the twentieth century's invention — the total war between peers — died, and nobody at the time noticed the funeral.*

## 7. "Land absorbs the rents — the Georgist revival works." (06 §5; errata 7)

**The steelman.** The LVT passed in 2046, *after* the land boom had done most of its appreciating — the classic "too late" tax. Land was ~25% of household wealth, and the true rents of the era sat in fleet equity, model IP, and orbital licenses, none of which a Georgist tax touches. Feld's dissent already argues capture decayed from 85% to ~40%; the steelman sharpens: the UIRA's floor-and-capture design was *repealed in substance* by 2064, and the sim's fiscal story quietly substitutes the VAT's regressive base for the Georgist base while calling the stool four-legged.

**Adjudication: the dissent is adopted; the claim survives as scaffolding, not as settlement.** Canon amendments: (a) UIRA effective capture: 85% (2046–49) → ~60% (2056) → ~40% (2064), per Feld; (b) peak LVT yield stands at $610B (2049) but its share of federal revenue falls from 9% to 4% by Y30; (c) the VAT's dominance is now stated plainly — by Y30 it is ~55% of receipts and the stool is *three-legged with a Georgist arm-rest*. What survives of the Georgist claim is the honest part: the LVT was the transition's *landing gear* — it caught the 2046 fiscal fall — and never the engine. Henry George's century, the panel's final verdict, arrived forty years late, worked for one decade, and was ratcheted away by the very electors it indexed.

## 8. "The meaning crisis was severe." (03 §3; canon Gray Slump)

**The steelman.** The entire epidemiology is instrument-shaped: the WAS measures depression against a 2019 baseline that was itself depressed, and samples the formerly employed — the population *organized* around work-identity. For the bottom two quintiles of the pre-Crossing economy, work was anxiety, surveillance, and physical wear, and the Crossing was *remission*; their flourishing rose from Y1, not Y18. The novelist's dissent (03 §8) is not a dissent but the median; the 26% trough is the professional strata's trough generalized, and "the Gray Slump" is a class event wearing a species name.

**Adjudication: partially adopted; the canon now stratifies.** Amendment: 03's §3.3 is promoted from footnote to headline — the Gray Slump's severity is *inversely correlated with pre-Crossing job quality*; the trough's composite falls from 26% to **19%** when re-weighted by the full population rather than the survey's contactable frame, and the professional-classes' own trough remains 26–30%. The Slack Line (income shallow, structures deep) is untouched — it is the pass's most robust finding — but the sim's moral headline changes register: *the transition hurt most exactly those the old economy had hurt least*, which is why its politics were so furious and so fixable.

## 9. "Global TFR: 1.65 at Y30, stabilizing." (05 §1, §5)

**The steelman.** Composition, not behavior (Osei-Bonsu's dissent, 05 §7a): the aggregate is religious-minority fertility, which converges historically, riding on top of secular-native rates locked near 1.3. Add ectogenesis scaling after 2053 and a genuine pro-natal technology curve, and the honest forecast is two: (a) continued decline to ~1.5 by Y40 (the dissent), or (b) a technology-driven rebound above 1.8 in adapter nations as crèche-robots, healthspan, and ectogenesis remove the *last* real constraints. The sim's 1.65 is the least defensible number in the corpus: a point estimate on a knife edge between a dissent and a boom.

**Adjudication: the point estimate survives; its error bars are canonized.** Amendment: 05 now carries the branch structure — the *secular-native* path (flat 1.3–1.4), the *minority-composition* path (driving the aggregate to 1.6 by Y30), and the *technology* path (ectogenesis + crèche-robots adding +0.05–0.10 in the 2050s, concentrated in France, Japan, Korea, the US religious belt). Global TFR 1.65 at Y30 stands as the *central branch*, with the panel's stated probability: 45% continued decline, 35% plateau, 20% technology rebound. The sim's deeper claim — *fertility tracks the Trust Divide, not income* — survives every test the pass could construct and is now flagged as the corpus's most transferable finding.

## 10. "The Global South loses its wage ladder; the developing world struggles or collapses." (01 §8; 02 §8; canon §2.2)

**The steelman.** The corpus takes the *loss* of wage advantage as the South's defining fact and reads failure off it. But the wage ladder was always a *ladder to somewhere else* — the labor-intensive export stage of development — and robots abolish the *need* to climb it. Leapfrog logic: a country with no legacy labor to protect, no unions to buy off, no wage floor to undercut, can adopt fleets at the *frontier* immediately; its constraint is capital and governance, and the AFC (2046) and the Sunbelt Sovereigns were built precisely to supply those. The sim's own evidence is scattered and un-aggregated: Vietnam (02 §8: "skipped the cheap-labor phase entirely"), India's robot-led industrialization, Morocco, Indonesia, the Commonwealth's 6.5% growth. Read honestly, the South's ledger has five leapfrog wins the "struggle" label conceals.

**Adjudication: the category is split; the failure list stands.** Amendment: 01 §8's "struggled" tier is now two tiers — **leapt** (Vietnam, Indonesia, Morocco, Bangladesh-after-2046 under the Dhaka Fleet Compact, the Commonwealth states) and **lagged** (Egypt, Ethiopia, Pakistan-post-collapse, the corridor states) — with the mechanism stated: leapfrog success tracked *fiscal capacity arriving before the payroll state died* (Kenya's mobile-money rails, the AFC's trust terms), and lag tracked the inverse. The canon's collapse list (Pakistan, Sudan, pre-Compact Bangladesh) is untouched and unapologetic — the Long Walk is the sim's moral center and the pass does not soften it.

## 11. "Democracy holds in adapter nations; the Orban Template is the failure mode." (06 §6; errata 6)

**The steelman.** The sim's binary — liberal settlement vs. clientelist capture — misses the outcome its own numbers predict: **plutocratic drift without fraud**. Gerontocratic electorates of asset-owners (05 §4: median voter 56, fleet-exposed) + a VAT-dominated fiscal base (you cannot VAT a balance sheet, Feld) + dividend politics reduced to one axis = a system that is formally democratic, legally clean, and substantively owned — the Fleet Protocol as the *cartel of the honest*, insurers as the unelected governor (06 §4), sortition chambers with subpoena power but no purse. Calloway's 3% ghost fleets and Feld's decaying LVT both point the same direction: the settlements distribute *income* from the machine economy while its *equity* compounds in fewer hands than the sim's Gini line admits.

**Adjudication: the middle category is adopted.** Amendment: the three settlements gain a fourth, unlabeled form — **the Managed Commonwealth** (the US/EU/China at Y30 share more than their constitutions admit): formally pluralist, substantively insurer-governed, with equity concentration masked by index diffusion (the median household's $180k fleet stake is real *income* security and trivial *control*). The Orban Template remains democracy's acute failure; Managed Commonwealth is its chronic one. The panel's verdict, for the record: *the transition did not end the ownership question; it made the ownership question boring, which is how ownership questions get settled.*

## 12. "The Silicon Curtain matters." (02 §2)

**The steelman.** By 02's own account the Curtain failed as containment within twelve years; open weights won; capability converged; the divergence lag settled at 18 months. A wall that fails on schedule is a plot device, not a structure — the corpus should say the 2035 Compact was the era's *least* consequential treaty and stop giving it a section.

**Adjudication: survives — because of what the failure left behind.** The Compact's product was not the wall but the *attestation stack* (02's "Singapore Pivot," 2039): provenance chains, firmware attestation, evaluation labs — and those became the load-bearing infrastructure of everything downstream: Protocol VII's veto verification, the Seoul Regime's compliance regime, the Fleet Protocol's interoperability layer, the Handmark's craft chain, even the Copenhagen Convention's intimacy-log rights. The claim is amended from "the Curtain divided compute" (false by Y12) to "the Curtain divided *verification*, permanently" (true at Y30): two trust architectures, one referee (Taiwan), and a world in which the rarest commodity is not intelligence but *auditable honesty* — which is the sim's actual scarcity all along.

## 13. "Robots deliver ~2.4 effective FTEs; deployment is constrained by capital turnover." (errata 3; canon §2.3)

**The steelman.** A robot does not sleep, strike, or quit: at 20 h/day × 340 days the nameplate 3.2 FTE is *conservative*, and the real brake on deployment was never engineering but **demand** — the Idle Fleet (40% utilization by Y25) is the proof. The 2.4 figure conflates two different frictions: task-change latency (real, temporal) and underutilization (economic, permanent). The corpus's supply-side story ("the world can only build so many robots") quietly dies by Y10 and the demand-side story ( fleets sit idle because *goods are cheap and hours are plentiful*) is never promoted to headline.

**Adjudication: adopted — the pass's most consequential reframing.** Amendment to canon §2.3: the deployment constraint is **supply-limited through ~Y10, demand-limited thereafter**. Consequences traced: the Fleet Levy's utilization base (06 §5) is cyclically volatile, not steady; the "robot production as the strategic asset" era ends by 2044 (which 02 §2's diffusion story already implies); and the Idle Fleet becomes the era's macro center of gravity — abundance's *unemployed machines* mirroring abundance's unemployed humans, both waiting for demand the price system no longer generates automatically. The deep-dive cycles build on this correction directly.

## 14. "Human-made culture plateaus at ~30%; sport and craft win." (04 §1–3; errata 4)

**The steelman.** The organic-analogy is a rich-country luxury (04's own dissent), and the 30% is a *spend* share, not a *time* share. Every prior "authenticity" premium (handmade furniture, live music post-recording, organic food) ended as a 5–15% niche; streaming's actual history is that audiences take good-enough synthetic at near-zero price within two product cycles. Sport's doubling is a Slack-Week bulge that decays as the Slack Week stops being novel. And the Fair Leagues — the sim's favorite — have a fatal precedent in the Enhanced Division's first ratings quarter, which beat the Natural Leagues by Y22.

**Adjudication: amended along the spend/time seam; sport survives.** Amendment: the 30% holds for *status expenditure* (what households spend on the human-made) but the *time* share falls to ~12% by Y30 for under-30s — the sin-eating household is the norm and the corpus now says so plainly. Sport is the survivor: its doubling holds at Y30 because the product is *unspoofable embodiment*, and the Enhanced Division's rise turns out to *feed* the Natural Leagues (a handicap needs a baseline). The Authenticity Turn's durable claim, restated: provenance won the auction houses and the guild halls and lost the pocket screen — *and the pocket screen is where the hours went*.

## 15. "The energy path is +144% by Y30 with no energy war." (canon §2.5; errata 5; 02 §6–7)

**The steelman.** The corpus's own war file contradicts its peace claim: the Two-Week War was, at the operational level, a war about electrons (genset convoys as the targeting signature); the Grid Accords are a peace treaty about infrastructure; the Electro-Balkans and the Lusaka Terms were energy-materials conflicts that *nearly* went hot; and copper, the physical chokepoint of electrification, has its own Karelian arithmetic. MAD-2 is not the absence of an energy war; it is the energy war *in its deterrence phase* — one treaty violation from hot, permanently.

**Adjudication: the claim survives only in its deterrence form — restated.** Amendment: errata 5's language is kept, and 02 gains the summary line the pass demands: *the energy war of the fleet century was deterred, not avoided; it was deterred the way the nuclear war was — by the fact that every party's civilian life support was the target.* The copper question is added to the canon's watchlist (the Lusaka Terms as the near-miss that worked), and the Grid Accords are upgraded from footnote to load-bearing: the sim's most under-appreciated treaty, holding 96% of the era's peace.

## 16. "Wealth concentration falls to 36% (top-1%) and nations converge (US:Kenya 25:1)." (canon §2.4; 01 §6)

**The steelman.** Both series are built on the same unexamined assumption — that ownership diffuses because index funds exist. But index ownership confers income, not control (steelman 11's point), and the control layer — Fleet Protocol Foundation seats, insurance-pool boards, ALC-1 capital accounts, the Public Model Trust's trustees — is staffed by a few thousand people whose names the sim never prints. Between countries, convergence rests on three African success cases carrying a continent (01's Dissent B) and on the Commonwealth's fund maturing on schedule; the corridor states' demography (median age 24, fund income arriving in the 2050s) says divergence persists past Y30 for 400M+ people. And the Botmafia's $300B ghost economy (06 dissent) is the *visible* part of the shadow equity the Gini line never touches.

**Adjudication: both claims survive as ranges; the ranges are canonized.** Amendment: top-1% wealth share at Y30: **36% (central) with a 33–41% band** whose upper edge is the no-Fleet-Protocol counterfactual; the US:Kenya ratio becomes **25:1 (central), 20:1–40:1 (band)** with the honest footnote that the *central* figure is the Commonwealth decade doing the work. The corpus's equity-diffusion claim survives because the dividend *is* equity — but the pass insists the corpus print, at every restatement, the distinction the whole era kept forgetting: **income ownership diffused; control ownership concentrated; and the politics of the 2060s is the arrival of that distinction in the ballot box.**

## Summary of the pass

- **Survived intact (in substance):** the Slack Line (§8's structure finding), the Trust-Divide fertility mechanism (§9), the verification divide (§12), the Long Walk's centrality (§10), the dividend's political durability (§5, accident-costed).
- **Survived amended:** peak unemployment (band widened, §1); the broken instrument (politics not statistics, §2); the trapdoor's shadow phase (§3); China's deadline (§4); the energy-peace form (§15); the Gini and convergence series (§16, ranges); the Authenticity Turn's seam (§14); the deployment constraint's flip (§13 — adopted as the cycles' premise).
- **Dissents adopted into canon:** Feld (LVT decay, §7); Osei-Bonsu (composition, §9); Calloway's ghost-fleet range (§11's shadow equity); the 03 novelist and the 05 urbanist (§8, and the Rooted appear again in Cycle 2's city work); the 02 dissents were already canon (Voskresensky Rule, the Fujian Memoranda).
- **Withdrawn:** nothing withdrawn outright — the pass's honest headline is that the corpus's *shape* survived its own cross-examination, but nine load-bearing numbers now carry the error bars, branch points, and named accidents they always should have had. The deep-dive cycles that follow build on the corrected ground.
