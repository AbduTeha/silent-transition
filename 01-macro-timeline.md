# PHASE 1 — THE MACRO TIMELINE, YEAR 0 TO YEAR 30
### Panel: macroeconomists, labor economists, an economic historian, a development economist, a fiscal-policy analyst

*All canon numbers are defined in README §2. This file is the spine: every other file hangs on it. Dates are absolute; indicators are defined once and never redefined.*

---

## 1. The broken instrument

The first thing the panel agrees on is that **GDP stops measuring anything useful within four years of the Crossing**, and the failure to notice this in real time is itself a cause of the political chaos of Y0–Y9.

Nominal GDP is a sum of market transactions. The Crossing does not shrink transactions — it *deflates their prices*. A warehouse that ran on 400 humans at $38/hour loaded runs on 90 GPRs at $3.80/hour. Output identical; nominal value-added collapses; the statisticians record contraction while the economy delivers the same tonnage. Between Y0 and Y5, US nominal GDP oscillates between −1% and +2% annualized while the Gross World Output index (consumption-equivalent, the panel's preferred instrument) rises from 100 to 118. Hedonic adjustments that had been academic curiosities become the difference between panic and comprehension, and they arrive three years late because the BEA's methodologies committee meets quarterly.

The panel therefore reports every era twice: what the instruments said, and what was actually happening. Future historians' name for the whole period — **the Silent Transition** — comes from the 2051 BIS post-mortem *The Price of Everything, the Value of Nothing*, whose central exhibit was the divergence of the two series. There was no announcement because there was never an event to announce — only a price crossing, then another, sector by sector, each one locally rational, each one invisible in aggregate until the aggregate broke.

**The Five Residues.** Why any human labor survives at all in a world of parity-priced robots and AGI-grade software. The panel treats these as the load-bearing walls of the entire simulation:

1. **Liability and trust.** Where errors are catastrophic and adjudicable — surgery, aviation, adjudication itself, childcare — institutions retain humans as *accountability anchors* regardless of cost. A model can decide; a human must be sue-able.
2. **Dexterity tails.** The long tail of unstructured physical environments (a 1970s Korean basement, a fishing trawler in a storm, a child's bedroom after a birthday) keeps human floor-level employment alive through the mid-2040s, then falls to teleoperated robots.
3. **Human-preference demand.** Once wages no longer bind, a large class of demand *re-prices upward* for the human version: care, hospitality, performance, craft, presence. This is not nostalgia; it is revealed preference once the price system stops screaming that human time is worthless.
4. **Oversight.** The entire Audit State (06, §3) — judges, attestors, ethicists, verifiers — exists because societies conclude that *checking the machines must be a human act for the check to bind morally*.
5. **Politics.** Make-work programs (the Civil Works Emergency, Boone's American Rebuild, India's Right to Contribution) are not economic policy; they are legitimacy policy. They persist because they work at being politics, not because they work at being jobs.

## 2. ERA I — THE SQUEEZE (Y0–Y3: 2034–2037)

*Theme: everything is still legal, employed, and priced normally — while the floor tilts.*

**Y0 — 2034.** The Crossing (March): in eleven US warehouses and two Shenzhen factories, GPR fully-loaded cost falls below the median human loaded wage for equivalent tasks. Nobody outside supply-chain circles notices; the jobs are already half-vacant (US warehouse turnover had run 60%/yr for a decade). The **Junior Freeze** begins within weeks — entry-level white-collar hiring drops 34% year-over-year as AGI-grade software absorbs analyst, junior-lawyer, junior-developer, and customer-service work. The US unemployment rate is 4.5% and the *Leading Indicators of Displacement* (a OECD dashboard almost nobody reads) cross their first thresholds. Services exporters feel it first: Indian IT services bookings fall 9% in Q3, the first tremor of the **Service Export Cliff**.

**Y1 — 2035.** Trucking pilots in Texas and Shandong; mining goes autonomous in Pilbara and Inner Mongolia (mining was pre-adapted — it had run remote operations for years). U-3: 6.5%. Retail waves begin in the UK and China (self-checkout had already destroyed the checkout job; the floor job goes next). November: the **Singapore Compute Compact** — the US, Japan, Korea, Netherlands, UK, and Taiwan agree export controls on frontier compute; Beijing answers within six weeks with the **Qwen Shock**, open-weights releases that put 90th-percentile capability in every hospital and ministry on Earth for free. The Curtain is born already leaking (02, §2). Consumer price index: −1.5% — the first deflation print that economists call "transitory" and are wrong about for three years.

**Y2 — 2036.** U-3: 9.5%. Ampersand launches **Margaret v1** ($19k lease): the home robot becomes a consumer category the way the smartphone did in 2007, and for the same reason — it is the first machine that does something the household actually does every day. US midterms: the displacement belt (warehouse counties, call-center counties, junior-professional suburbs) flips 31 House seats; the populist right captures the House on "Keep America Working" while losing the popular vote on every other issue. India launches the **National Robotics Mission** — the great irony of the sim's Global South: the countries that lose wage advantage first are the ones that pivot to building the machines. The EU publishes the Second Draghi Report; its recommendations (buy fleets collectively, co-determine them, tax the rents) become the Brussels Compromise four years later. Global installed base: 18M.

**Y3 — 2037.** U-3: 13%. **The Labor-Debt Crunch (May 2037).** The decade's consumer credit had been underwritten by payroll continuity — auto loans, credit cards, BNPL, and $14T of mortgage exposure priced against two-income households with glossy employment histories. Displacement cascades through the securitization stack the way subprime did in 2008, but *faster and flatter* — there is no housing-asset boom to cushion it, because the same shock is deflating wages and prices. The Federal Reserve's response is the constitutional experiment of the decade: the **People's Tap**, direct central-bank transfers to households (helicopter money institutionalized), under emergency authorities that survive two Supreme Court challenges by 4–4–1. President Okafor's **Civil Works Emergency** (2.5M positions: rivers, forests, care queues, inspection backlogs) is the first explicit make-work of the era. China consolidates its robot industry into **CGRG** — the state does in one decree what the US needs eleven years and an antitrust case to do. Global installed base: 35M. The world is officially in a "structural recession" that every subsequent commission will conclude was actually a *transition observed through a broken instrument*.

## 3. ERA II — THE BREAKING (Y4–Y9: 2038–2043)

*Theme: the labor market's remaining load-bearing walls fail in sequence; politics radicalizes; the fiscal state confronts its own arithmetic; the first wars and crashes of the new age.*

**Y4 — 2038.** U-3: 17%; U-6 (broad underemployment): 26%. The **Long Walk** begins: interior South Asia displaces ~90M over the next eight years as the Service Export Cliff, the garment collapse (Bangladesh loses 3.1M jobs in 18 months — four-fifths of its export earnings), and heat-driven agricultural failure compound. Excess mortality over the period: ~1.9M, mostly water, heat, and cholera — the sim's greatest humanitarian wound, and the one the history books of 2087 treat with the most shame, because it was *forecast* (the IMF's 2036 asymmetric-shocks paper had it to within 20%). The **Mediterranean Grid Compact** signs: EU capital, Gulf capital, Maghreb sun, HVDC under the sea. Asset prices explode — **the Land Grab Decade** begins in earnest: land, compute, fleet equities, and anything robot-adjacent re-rate 40%/yr while wages deflate. The Catholic Church publishes *Lux et Labor*. China launches the **Wuxi Experiment**: a 12M-person city-scale trial of the Provision System (state fleet dividends as points + cash).

**Y5 — 2039.** **Peak displacement: US U-3 hits 20.5% in May.** Participation has collapsed to 48% — the difference is the *discouraged dimension*, invisible in U-3 and screaming in every other dataset. The **Grid Riots** (summers 2039–40, Detroit, Chicago, Lyon, Sheffield, Shenzhen's dongqu, Bengaluru): not food riots — the abundance side of the ledger is real, calories and clothing are cheaper than any time in history — but *usefulness riots*: attacks on robot deployment sites, the "Hands Up" movement's mass occupations, a half-million-person march in Chicago whose banner ("**WE ARE NOT THE RESIDUE**") becomes the era's emblem. China nationalizes the Wuxi results into the **Provision System**. India's Right to Contribution Act (2040) enrolls 90M in make-work that works badly and then moderately. The World Affect Survey begins recording the **Gray Slump**. Global installed base: 90M — at 2.4 effective FTE each, roughly 216M FTE-equivalents, about 7% of world labor hours, and yet concentrated so selectively (logistics, retail backrooms, code) that the *felt* displacement is triple the aggregate.

**Y6 — 2040.** U-3: 19%. The **Brussels Compromise**: the EU's three-pillar settlement (fleet co-determination on the German model, the EU **Citizen Fleet** — fleet shares allocated per capita like Alaska's oil fund — and a €900/month floor). It is the sim's most underrated polity move: by buying *ownership shares* rather than income, Europe converts its most restive cohort into shareholders and co-managers. US midterms: the **Steelman Coalition** — left redistributionists and right family-populists, united on dividends and tariffs, agreeing on nothing else — takes the House on "Dividend Now." Meridian-7 precursor stresses appear in quant funds running autonomous strategies. Global TFR bottoms: 1.7.

**Y7 — 2041.** The annus horribilis. March: **Meridian-7**, an autonomous hedge-fund cascade — three models each hedging against the others' hedging — wipes $2.1T in 36 hours; the circuit breakers work, the *legitimacy* breakers do not; the Meridian Rule (2042) imposes fiduciary duties on autonomous financial agents and quietly becomes the template for AI law everywhere. November: the **Two-Week War** (02, §4): Russia's machine-army gamble in the Baltic, 11,000 dead, the Kaliningrad Clock near-miss. Curtis Boone (R) is elected US president in November 2044 on "America Rebuilds" — but in 2041 he is still a senator blaming Okafor. Global installed base: 200M.

**Y8 — 2042.** U-3: 15%. **Geneva Protocol VII** — lethal autonomy requires a human veto with verified latency (the "human hand rule" for war). France's **Statut Universel** ends the pension wars by converting the pension into a universal presence payment. The first US attempt at a Human Essentiality Amendment fails in the Senate (the fleet lobbies call it "the Luddite Amendment"). The People's Tap is wound down; deficits peak at 11% of GDP; interest rates stay at 1.2% anyway — the robot-savings glut swallows the issuance (the "savings tsunami" that the 2043 Jackson Hole consensus calls the most underappreciated macro fact of the era).

**Y9 — 2043.** U-3: 13.5%. Japan's Kaigo-Robo program covers 50% of care tasks — the first polity to cross into post-scarcity *care*, and the first to discover that care-robot coverage is the single most popular policy in the democratic world (73% approval, including among caregivers). China's Provision System strains (errata #1): Wuxi-style riots in Harbin; the hukou equalization decree (2043) is Beijing's answer — full urban services for migrants, the largest single redistribution in Chinese history. World population peaks: 9.2 billion. Global installed base: 320M.

## 4. ERA III — THE SETTLEMENT (Y10–Y15: 2044–2049)

*Theme: the political question — who owns the machines — gets answered, differently, in each bloc; the answer becomes the new constitution of daily life.*

**Y10 — 2044.** U-3: 12.5%; participation 40%. Boone's "American Rebuild" make-work (3M positions) runs alongside the largest military demobilization in history (conscription is extinct worldwide by this year). The US fiscal gap peaks: income+payroll, 60% of federal revenue at Y0, is down to 34% and falling 3 points a year. The arithmetic is now undeniable: the state must tax *the machines* or stop existing.

**Y11 — 2045.** The Lusaka Terms: African mineral states force local beneficiation (cobalt, copper, lithium processed on-continent) — the first time the terms-of-trade move *toward* the South since the Crossing, because robots need materials and materials are the one thing the South still has. *US v. OmniFleet* is filed: the antitrust case of the century, against the largest Fleet Consortium (41% of US robot-hours).

**Y12 — 2046.** The hinge year. **March: the American Dividend Act** — Marsh's grand bargain: **Abundance VAT (14%)** + **Land Value Tax** (3% of assessed land value; the Georgist revival is formalized) + a **Fleet Levy** ($1.10/robot-hour, phased 2048) fund a universal adult dividend (**the Copper Card**, first payments January 2047, $10,200/adult/yr), plus the Universal Coverage Act's healthcare path, plus a statutory promise that the dividend *rises with fleet productivity* — which converts every future productivity gain into a citizens' claim, and every fleet owner into a counterparty of the whole society. June: the **Lagos Accords** — the African Fleet Cooperative (AFC) under Afreximbank: nations lease fleets in trust, 30% of fleet income ring-fenced into sovereign futures funds (the Abuja Rule). Kigali opens the Protocol City. Delaware invents the **ALC-1** (Limited Agent Charter). Global installed base: 700M.

**Y13 — 2047.** The **Fleet Divestiture Act** and the settlement of *US v. OmniFleet*: no divestiture of robots — divestiture of *control planes*. The **Fleet Protocol** (mandatory interoperability of fleet-management systems, open core under the Fleet Protocol Foundation) is the sim's "TCP/IP for labor": any fleet can run on any dispatch layer, which converts the oligopoly into a commodity market within six years and is the single most consequential regulatory act of the era. The **Human Essentiality Amendment** is ratified (human adjudication, human authentication, the right to contribute, assembly). The **Seoul Regime** signs on autonomous weapons. China's Hangzhou succession installs the technocrats. **Hong Kong Fleet Convention** harmonizes fleet levies — the first global tax treaty since the ocean-shipping accords.

**Y14 — 2048.** US U-3: 11%. The dividend economy's consumer boom is now the world's demand engine: Copper Card spending is 9% of US consumption and 100% of the growth in bottom-half consumption. The Fusion Mirage peaks (wrongly announced room-temperature superconductors and fusion "within five years" — both real, both a decade late). Global installed base: 900M.

**Y15 — 2049.** US U-3: 11%; participation 30%. Dividend: $11,900. The **Nigerian Robotics Commonwealth** — the AFC's flagship polity — is the Global South's proof case: not rich, but *converging* (GDP/capita growth 6.5%/yr for four straight years). Global TFR: 1.6. The World Affect Survey crosses back above its 2034 baseline in every adapter nation. The era of *breaking* is over; the era of *reorganization* begins. Global installed base: 1.1B.

## 5. ERA IV — REORGANIZATION (Y16–Y22: 2050–2056)

*Theme: the settled dividend polities turn from survival to construction — and discover the next frontier is not production but allocation, healthspan, and meaning.*

**2050.** US Universal Coverage Act completes the healthcare transition. The "Standing Election" phenomenon: municipal turnout exceeds 60% in cities with sortition chambers — local politics has become a status arena (03, §4). Global installed base: 1.3B.

**2051.** First commercial fusion baseload (Hefei; the US follows 2053). The Ectogenesis Act (France) detonates the decade's culture war (Cycle 2, thread 8). Labor share of income: 22%, and *stable* — the Five Residues have stopped shrinking; the floor is visible.

**2052.** The **Healthspan Dividend**: first longevity-drug cohorts (TAME-successor trials) deliver 4-year median healthspan gains — retirement systems recast themselves as *healthspan systems*. Thibodeaux (R) wins the presidency in November on "Double the Dividend" — the dividend check is now the third rail of US politics, exactly as Social Security was for a century. The Handicap Wars peak in sport governance (04, §3).

**2053–2055.** The Great Sorting completes (05, §3): the Median City Renaissance census wave; the Monastery Belt repurposing programs peak; urbanization 78% in adapter nations. US top-1% wealth share: 38%. The Minjiang Understanding (2055) stabilizes the Taiwan Strait into "ambient peace" — joint compute ventures, mutual grid transparency, and the quiet obsolescence of the invasion plan.

**2056.** "Double the Dividend" landslide (Thibodeaux, 412 EVs). Global installed base: 2.2B. GWO index: 310.

## 6. ERA V — THE LONG EQUILIBRIUM (Y23–Y30: 2057–2064)

*Theme: the new normal — abundance with an underutilized machine fleet, a 20% employment rate that nobody calls unemployment anymore, politics organized around the dividend-craft-status triangle.*

**2057–2060.** The **Idle Fleet** becomes a first-order economic fact: 2.6B installed GPRs, average utilization ~40%. Robot ownership has diffused — the median US household holds ~$180k of fleet exposure through the mandatory 401-swap ("Robot Index") and sovereign funds — so the Idle Fleet is not a political crisis but a scheduling problem, and fleet owners respond by pushing the experience economy (the demand side needs humans *doing things* to sell them things to do). Yaw (D) wins 2060. US U-3: 8%; but the meaningful number is the **Post-Employment Rate**: 79% of adults neither employed nor seeking — living on dividends, family production, Third Structures, and craft income, and (the surveys insist) no worse for it on average than their employed grandchildren will be.

**2061–2064.** Yaw's sortition reforms (2063) add a 400-member civic assembly chosen by lot to the US system — the Audit State formalized at the constitutional level. World population: 8.9B and falling 0.3%/yr. Global installed base: 3.2B, plateauing. Labor share: 19%. GWO: 340. Between-country inequality: the US:Kenya GDP/capita ratio has fallen from 60:1 to 25:1 — the first great convergence, driven not by aid or trade but by the fact that robots made *location* nearly worthless for production and left only materials, sun, and governance as differentiators (Africa holds three of the four best endowments of the new economy: sun, minerals, young people; it lacks only the governance, which the Commonwealth wave is slowly supplying).

The 2064 BIS retrospective closes with the sentence that gives the whole period its name: *"The transition was silent because it was priced, not decreed. No cabinet ever voted on the end of work; the end of work was an externality of forty million procurement decisions. The shouting came afterward, and it was about the division of the proceeds — which is to say, about everything."*

## 8. THE THREE FATES — WHICH NATIONS ADAPT, STRUGGLE, COLLAPSE, OR CAPTURE

The panel's five preconditions for a good transition — **fiscal capacity** (can the state tax machines?), **energy position** (does it own or cheaply buy electrons?), **compute access** (is it inside or outside the Curtains?), **governance speed** (can it restructure law inside five years?), and **social trust** (will the losers believe the winners?) — predict national fates better than income, institutions, or democracy.

**ADAPTED (13):** US (dividend settlement, Y12 — three years late, one civil-riot period early), China (Provision System — fastest deployment, hardest legitimacy problem, per errata #1), Japan (care-first, demography-first; the most orderly transition in the sample), Korea, Taiwan (survives the window), Singapore, Norway (extends the oil fund into the Botfund), Switzerland (neutrality + fleet funds), UAE, Saudi (Verdant), Germany (co-determination exports), France (Statut Universel), Estonia (e-governance does for fleets what it did for services).

**STRUGGLED THEN CONVERGED (8):** UK (a decade of austerity-first error, then the 2047 Fleet Fund), EU-periphery (Spain, Portugal, Greece: Grid Union transfers + tourism's collapse), Poland (robot-manufacturing hub by Y12), India (the worst aggregate exposure of any democracy — 700M workers — and the most surprising save: robots let it industrialize *without* the labor-intensive stage, and "Crafted in India" human-premium exports replace services by Y15), Indonesia, Vietnam (the assembly boom dies Y2, the robot-assembly boom begins Y3), Mexico (nearshoring boom Y0–2, bust Y3–5 when robots onshore fully, Care-Commons boom Y10+), Brazil (soy fleet automation plus dividend-lite).

**COLLAPSED OR FRACTURED (9):** Pakistan (the
 garrison state's fiscal base was payroll; it never had one again), Bangladesh (garment collapse → the 2041 Delta Emergency), Sudan, several Sahel states (the Displacement Corridor; the Midday Rings partially save Senegal and Mauritania), Angola, Iraq (no fund, no plan), Venezuela-successor states, plus two "rich collapses": the UAE-alikes that bet everything on hosting *other people's* compute and lost it when orbital constellations (2050s) broke the ground-station moat — Abu Dhabi alone survives by buying into the winners.

**AUTHORITARIAN CAPTURE (4):** Hungary (the Orban Template, 2043: dividend-for-loyalty clientelism — the innovation that terrified every democracy's opposition), the Philippines (2046), Russia (post-war "battery state": aging, militarized, shrinking, a Chinese resource appendix after the Yenisei Arrangement), Iran (compute-sanctioned into technological priesthood). The Template is the sim's most-exported bad idea: nine further attempts, four of which stuck long enough to matter.

*Why the "why" matters:* every adapted polity did the same three things in the same order — (1) taxed the machines before the income tax base died (the fiscal inversion), (2) converted the restive cohort into *owners* (shares, not just checks), (3) funded meaning-structures, not just income (the Association Dividend lesson, 04 §7). Every collapsed polity failed at least #1 and #2. No polity survived failure at all three.

## 9. PANEL DISSENTS (recorded for Phase 8 cross-examination)

- **Dissent A (monetarist):** the panel's fiscal gaps are understated; the People's Tap only worked because the savings glut held rates down, and a less credible Fed in a less key-currency country would have faced a currency crisis, not a smooth tap. The US path is not exportable; the EU path is; most of the "collapsed" list is really "ran the US path without the dollar."
- **Dissent B (development):** the panel's convergence story is too sunny about Africa by Y30 — three success cases (Nigeria, Rwanda, Kenya) are carrying the narrative of a continent, and the Displacement Corridor's demographics (median age 24, no fleet income until the AFC matures in the 2050s) say the divergence persists past the simulation horizon for at least 400M people.
- **Dissent C (labor):** participation numbers flatter the outcome by counting "craft income" and Third Structures activity that is economically marginal; the panel should track *claims on the machine economy* per capita, and by that measure half the world's adults are still tenants of the fleet economy, not shareholders, at Y30.
