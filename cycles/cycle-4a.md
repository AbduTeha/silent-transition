## THREAD 31 — The attention economy in numbers

**The market.** The creator economy's transition-era numbers, from the **Audience Bureau** (est. 2049, the measurement consortium the platforms were forced to fund after the "viewability wars"): **61M people in adapter nations earn on audiences by Y15** (03 §4's "tens of millions," made exact) — median earnings among *earning* creators: $21,000/yr (against the dividend floor, a wage supplement, not a wage); the 90th percentile: $140,000; the top 0.1% capture **31% of all creator income** — attention inequality, as 03 §4 forecast, running ~3× income inequality, the most concentrated legitimate market on Earth.

**The 1,000-fans arithmetic, verified.** The slogan became literal: at Y15 adapter-nation prices, a creator needing $35,000/yr clears it at 1,170 patrons averaging $2.50/month — and the Slack Week's patron surplus (the billion affluent evenings, 04 §2) made patronage the *default* consumption act of the middle class: 44% of dividend households sustain at least one creator monthly. The structural finding the Bureau added: **niches don't saturate** — because attention spent on one creator does not subtract from another (unlike goods), the market's capacity grows with the audience's free hours; total creator income rose every year of the era, in every country that had a dividend.

**The dysfunctions, priced.** The Attention Aristocracy's parasocial governance (03 §4's mob adjudication) institutionalized into the **clout courts** (2046+; platform-funded arbitration of creator-fan disputes — the system's docket: 1.9M cases/yr by Y20, 84% resolved without escalation); the mid-tail's desperation economics (the "clout crimes" — manufactured feuds, grief-baiting — met by the platforms' authenticity tier and, culturally, by the Audience Bureau's **Parasocial Health Index**, which advertisers now buy against: brands pay premiums for audiences whose attachment is *healthy* — the metric's absurdity is the point, and it works); and the endgame nobody predicted: the **sindication** — the top creators' audience-wealth funds (the "clout co-ops"), by which the Aristocracy's best members converted fame into fleet-index ownership for their communities — the status market's money learning, late, to buy the same assets as everyone else.

**The synthesis finding.** Thread 31's verdict, from the Bureau's decade review: the attention economy did not replace the labor economy; it *bridged* the dividend to the portfolio self — the median post-work life in 2064 earns ~$6,000/yr from attention, craft, and presence markets atop the $16,000 dividend, and the psychological literature (03's Slack Line) shows the earning matters less than the *audience*: being watched doing something you chose is the closest thing the fleet century sells to being needed.

## THREAD 32 — The Copper Card economy: a year in the dividend

**The money.** $10,200/adult/yr (Jan 2047) → $11,900 (2049) → $13,800 (2054) → $16,000 (2064), plus the VATback per-capita rebate and the bassinet bonus ($1,400/child under 6), on 262M adults (2064): **$4.4T of annual citizen cash flow** — 9% of GDP moving through 262M accounts, the largest recurring payment system in economic history. Where it goes (the Treasury's 2062 flow study, the "copper ledger"): housing 24% · food 14% · care and health out-of-pockets 11% · travel and experience 13% · education and formation 9% · craft and presence purchases (the human-premium belt) 8% · giving (religious, mutual aid, patronage) 7% · the Fleet Participation sleeve 5% (before opt-outs) · everything else 9%.

**The local multiplier.** The dividend's geography was its politics: the **dividend towns** — the rural and Rust Belt places that had lost payroll first — became the spending boom's epicenter, because a flat $16,000 is worth 2.3× more where the cost index is low: county-level studies (the Kansas Fed's 2053 series) show bottom-half consumption *rising fastest exactly where it had fallen furthest*, the "copper cross" on the map of America's comeback counties. The politics follow the arithmetic: the dividend's coalition is structurally rural-small-town-plus-urban-poor — the "copper belt" — which is why Double the Dividend (2056) ran up 412 electoral votes and why no major party has run against the card since 2050.

**The macro card.** Three second-order findings from a decade of natural experiments: (1) the **marginal propensity to consume** out of the dividend ran 0.71 — the demand engine of the Idle Fleet era, the reason the fleets stay utilized at all; (2) **giving did not collapse** — the 7% flow is *higher* as a share of income than pre-Crossing charity, concentrated in congregations and mutual aid: abundance, it turns out, did not end tithing, it nationalized the wallet and left the tithe local; (3) the **velocity anomaly** — dividend dollars turn over 1.4× faster than wage dollars did (the wallets that spend don't save like the wallets that earned), which is why the VAT's yield outran every forecast and why the "dividend-versus-everything" budget wars (06 §6) never quite materialized: the money came back through the consumption tax faster than the models said.

## THREAD 33 — The grid: market design when electrons are the wage

**The system.** 78,000 TWh at Y30 (canon): solar+wind+storage 61%, fission 18%, fusion 6%, hydro/geo/other 15%. Wholesale $0.012/kWh. Data centers and AI: ~9,000 TWh and plateaued (efficiency won); robot fleets ~12,000 TWh; the rest is electrified transport, heat, and industry. The design problem the era actually solved was not generation (the SDL pipeline and the Sunbelt solved that) but **firm capacity and trust** — and both answers are institutional.

**The firm-capacity auction.** The Sunbelt Sovereigns' product is *firmness*: the **Firm Capacity Exchange** (Abu Dhabi, 2046) auctions guaranteed-delivery blocks (the "9-nines" contracts: 99.99% delivery or the penalty is power) — storage fleets, fission, fusion, and orbital solar's dawn coverage underwriting what variable solar cannot. Price of firmness: $0.031/kWh delivered-guaranteed at Y30, against $0.008 for as-available electrons — the spread is the Grid Union's margin and the reason deserts and utilities print money while coastlines don't.

**Demand response for everything.** Thread 1's Labor Demand Response (2054) has its grid twin: **compute as a shock absorber** — the data centers' flexible workloads (training runs, render farms) bid into balancing markets, consuming surplus and standing down in scarcity, which is why the era's grids tolerate 70% variable penetration without storage buildout matching the forecasts. The 2059 **"the Grid is the Battery"** consensus: the cheapest storage is the interruptible load you already built.

**MAD-2 in operation.** The Grid Accords (2046, canon) get their operating detail: the **Nicosia early-warning center** (joint grid telemetry, the "signed ping" authentication of cycle-2b's register) processed 41 false-alarm de-escalations Y20–Y30; the **blackout treaty's** inspection regime (converter-station attestation, dual-key maintenance access); and the compliance mechanism that makes it hold — the pools (Thread 22) price *unattested* grid behavior out of insurance, so the treaty's enforcement is a premium schedule. The residual risk the file names honestly: the **microgrid sovereignty movement** (localities building islandable grids for resilience) is, from MAD-2's perspective, also *decoupling* — a thousand deliberate islands are a thousand fewer hostages to mutual disruption, and a thousand fewer reasons for the restraint the hostage logic provides. The 2062 Nicosia review calls this "the safety paradox of resilience" and has no answer, only a watch item.

## THREAD 34 — The guild economy: apprenticeship, craft, and the Handmark belt

**The system.** The guilds rebuilt faster than any institution of the 2040s: **9,400 certified guilds** in adapter nations by Y30 (craft, care-adjacent, stage, field, marine, restoration), 6.2M apprentices and 1.1M masters, under the Handmark Trust's standards and the Apprenticeship Acts' public funding (two days a week, ages 12–18, canon). Completion economics: 71% of apprentices complete (against vocational education's 41% at Y0) — the dividend removed the desperation drop-out, and the portfolio self removed the stigma; the median guild graduate's earnings: $29,400/yr craft income on top of the dividend, at 22 hours/week — the "half-time at double pay" life, institutionalized.

**The ladder.** Apprentice → journeyer → maker → master, with the **Witness Piece** at each rank (the attested solo work, the guild system's thesis project): the master's rank requires the Witness Piece *and* two apprentices brought to rank — the "grow the ladder behind you" rule, the guilds' answer to the gatekeeping risk (Thread 28), written into their charters after the 2051 **Lutherie Gate scandal** (a master's college caught selling ranks; dissolved; the rule universalized).

**The trade.** The Handmark belt's flows at Y30: **$410B/yr in certified-human trade** — the exports of the Global South's craft economy ("Crafted in India" $180B, the Ibadan film and textile belt, the Andean and East African guild exports, Portugal's azulejo revival) meeting the North's premium demand; the barbell of the Vermeer effect (04 §2) now visible as trade data: the attested top commands 8–30× the synthetic substitute, the unattested middle has ceded to the South what the North abandoned, and the *volume* story is unchanged (synthetic dominates everywhere — Thread 14's 12% time-share for under-30s). The guild economy's macro role, per the 2058 WTO-successor review: it is the fleet century's *development ladder* — the one export sector where the South's human endowment is the advantage, the wage premium flows South, and no robot can follow.

**The culture.** The guilds' unexpected export was not goods but *form*: the apprenticeship structure became the era's general education (the folkehøjskole layer, Thread 11), its governance became the sortition chambers' template, and its Watch Piece became the culture's proof-of-love — the attested gift (the bench, the violin, the shawl) as the era's highest non-vow commitment. The Cambridge History's line on the whole file: *"The twentieth century made work the thing you sold. The fleet century made work the thing you gave."*

## THREAD 35 — Aging's full economics: the Gray Dividend's balance sheet

**The dependency inversion.** The 2026 fear — aging societies as fiscal terror — died of arithmetic: with output decoupled from labor (labor share 19%) and care at $0.40/hr machine cost plus $41/hr human noticing, the old-age dependency ratio stopped being a solvency variable and became a *demand* variable: elders are the care economy's customer base, the Care Commons' residents, the crèche's volunteer second shift. The Gray Dividend's ledger (the 2060 OECD elderhood report): the +4-year healthspan (canon) is worth, in avoided care costs alone, ~$900B/yr across the OECD; the elder consumption boom (travel, presence markets, the Care Commons) is ~6% of adapter-nation GDP; the "grandparent dividend" — elders + margarets running the intergenerational crèche — supplies 31% of informal childcare at zero fiscal cost.

**The institutions.** The five-phase life's elderhood phase has real architecture now: the **Elder Commons** (the skill banks and mentorship exchanges — 4.1M active mentorships in the US alone), the Monastery Belt's care campuses (Thread 9; ~40M people, better served than at any point in their regions' history), the **Senior Colleges** (4.1M enrolled, cycle-2a), and the death interface: the Continuarian-adjacent services (Tended Selves, testamentary consent per Copenhagen) meeting the Stillstandsnacht-grade ceremony culture — the era's funerals are, by every survey, better than its births' christenings, and the corpus flags that asymmetry as data about the century's priorities.

**The estates.** The greatest wealth transfer in history is underway: the Lucky Retirees (born 1964–79, 03 §2.4) hold the land boom's gains and the index's compounding, and their estates are flowing — $14T/yr in the US by the 2060s — into exactly three channels: the index (inheritance default-invested in the Fleet Participation sleeve), the family (the multigenerational return's financing), and the institutions (the Third Structures' endowment wave: congregations, guilds, libraries, the "copper chapels"). The plutocratic-drift question (08 §11) gets its honest answer here: inheritance is concentrating *within* families while the default-investment floor socializes the flow — the Gini of the dead's decisions is higher than the Gini of the living's income, and the estate-tax fights of the 2060s are the next decade's fiscal war, pre-visible in every file that touches the gerontocratic electorate.

**The politics.** The Gray Dividend's voter: 56 years old, fleet-exposed, healthspan-optimizing, care-secure — the constituency that made the dividend untouchable and the care budgets untouchable and (the file's warning, per 05 §4 and 06 §6) everything else contested. The 2064 WAS data's one sore line: elders report the era's highest flourishing *and* its highest anxiety about the young — the "grandchildren worry" — which is the gerontocratic drift's psychological base, and the reason Thread 39's cohort data matters politically as much as clinically.
