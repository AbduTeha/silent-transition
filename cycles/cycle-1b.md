## THREAD 6 — Africa's two decades: minerals, the AFC, and the fleet leapfrog

**The mineral boom and its terms.** The fleet economy's input list reads like an African geological survey: copper, cobalt, lithium, bauxite, manganese, phosphate, plus the solar belt's land and sun. DRC + Zambian copperbelt output tripled Y0–Y8; the **Lusaka Terms (2045)** — negotiated under the credible threat of an export coordination council of eleven states — mandated 70% on-continent beneficiation of cobalt and copper cathode, setting the pattern (Indonesia had pioneered it with nickel in 2020; the Terms made it the continental standard). The boom's fiscal yield ($61B/yr continent-wide by Y10, vs $14B pre-Crossing) funded the Accords' preconditions. The boom's reversal risk arrived on schedule: sodium-ion cathodes (scale 2042) cut lithium demand growth by half — and the states that had diversified into *fleet income* (the AFC's logic) outperformed the pure mineral states by 3:1 in per-capita income growth Y12–Y20, which is the empirical foundation of the Abuja Rule's design.

**The African Fleet Cooperative.** The Lagos Accords (2046) created the century's most-copied Southern institution: **fleet-in-trust for equity**. Member states contribute mineral royalties, solar-land leases, and regulatory sandbox rights; the AFC (Afreximbank as trustee, AU secretariat as policy layer) leases robot fleets to national and sub-national co-ops at concessionary rates; **30% of fleet income is ring-fenced into national futures funds** (the Abuja Rule) that invest in global fleet equity — so that Africa's dividend, when it matures in the 2050s, is paid *by the same global fleet economy that skipped its wages*. The AFC's fleet pool reached 24M units by Y15 (largest single holder after CGRG), and its insurance is the **Lagos Mutual** (06 §4's third pool) — the first reinsurance pool outside the OECD, capitalized by the futures funds themselves.

**The flagship cases.** *Nigeria*: naira crisis (2040) → Church-Coop Grid (the Pentecostal and Aladura networks as last-mile energy and fleet-coop infrastructure — the state's weakness substituted by social trust, exactly 01 §8's mechanism) → **Robotics Commonwealth (2049)** → futures-fund maturity (2052: the first dividend cohorts, ₦-denominated, in the Eastern co-op states first) → Lagos at 28M and the Abuja Rule renegotiation fights (07, Adaeze's portfolio). *Rwanda*: the **Kigali Protocol City** (2046) — a 40-km² regulatory sandbox for fleet leasing, attestation services, and cross-stack certification; 400 fleet firms resident by Y15; "the Dubai of fleet services," including the Dubai problem (governance), which the AU's 2058 Kigali Charter (sandbox-wide HE-Amendment-equivalent rights) was written to fix. *Kenya*: mobile-money rails made it the world's cheapest place to administer a fleet levy (collection cost 0.4% of yield vs 2.8% US); Nairobi became the NACM Verification Registry's operating hub; per-capita income 4× real by Y30 — the convergence headline's main engine.

**The failures, kept honest.** The Sahel Displacement Corridor (2038–50): the Long Walk's western analog, 14M displaced, the region's pastoral economy destroyed by the same double shock; the **Midday Rings** — EU-funded solar-desalination city-belts (Dakar–Saint-Louis, Nouakchott, Ouagadougou ring) — housed 9M by Y20 and stood as the era's most expensive half-success. Sudan: state failure continuous. Ethiopia: 130M people, corridors of displacement, the fleet economy arriving in the 2050s at scale — a decade too late for the cohort that left. The continent's arithmetic at Y30: median age 24, 500M entering working age 2035–2055 with no wage ladder, futures funds maturing — the sim's greatest unresolved bet, and 01's Dissent B remains the standing caveat: three success cases are carrying a narrative.

## THREAD 7 — The Gray Slump at scale: the epidemiology file

**The instruments.** The World Affect Survey (Helsinki Global Affect Observatory; biennial; n≈2.4M/wave; 78 countries) plus the clinical registers (the "H-rate": diagnosed depression, per 03) plus the prescription and mortality series. Re-stratified per 08 §8: the **composite trough is 19%** (re-weighted to the full population), the professional strata's trough 26–30%, the bottom-two-quintiles' trajectory *falling from Y1* (remission, not recession — the novelist's dissent, promoted to headline).

**The numbers by year (adapter nations, composite/full-weighted):** Y0 18% · Y3 20% · Y5 22% · Y8 **19–26% (trough, by stratum)** · Y10 17% · Y12 18%→16% · Y15 15% · Y18 **14%** · Y20 13.5% · Y30 12%. Suicide: peak Y9 at ~40% above the Y0 baseline, **below baseline by Y15**, −22% vs baseline by Y20 — and the pass's key mechanism note: means-restriction policies did not move the curve; *structure* did (Third Structures density correlates −0.71 with the suicide decline across regions; income support alone, −0.12).

**Goalless Syndrome** (DSM-6, 2044: *Persistent Anhedonia Without Dysphoria*): 2–5% of prime-age adults at trough; 90% concentrated in the identity-invested strata; the Sheffield trial (2043, n=12,000, randomized against waitlist): the **Purpose Prescribing** protocol — prescribed, scheduled, witnessed activity from the Meaning Formulary (guild rotations, care shifts, conservation corps, sports officiating) — cut Goalless incidence among treated patients by a third (NNT 3.2) at **£2,100/patient-year**, which made it, per QALY, the most cost-effective mental-health intervention ever fielded. Japan's ikigai clinics ran the same mechanism at population scale (¥18,000/capita/yr; the lowest-stigma, highest-contact model; Korea copied within two years; the US copied it as the **Civic Corps**: 600k enrolled Y11, 1.5M/yr by Y12, stipend $9,800 + structure; the Corps' own RCT showed depression −22% vs dividend-only controls — the largest de facto mental-health program a state ever ran, designed as employment policy, memo'd internally as therapy).

**What failed, quantified:** awareness campaigns (null); **AI therapy at scale** (the 2044 Calmera "Empathy Slot" leak: the oversight layer itself synthetic, engagement optimization deepening dependency — the 2045 Human-in-the-Room licensure acts ended AI-first therapy in the OECD); cash-only transfers (WAS-flat); the "retraining decade" (2015–2035's answer: reskilling for jobs that did not exist to be reskilled into — the era's most expensive null result, $310B US cumulative).

**The despair belt.** No V: the collapsed-transition states' registers show depression *rising* through Y20 (scarcity never ended; the structures never arrived). The Walkers' coping systems — the mutual-aid camps, the reconstituted village, the contribution camps' informal structure — are the corpus's thinnest data and its most important gap; the 2060 *Lancet* review's verdict stands as the file's closing line: *"The Transition's mental-health record divides the world into the treated, the relieved, and the unrecorded. Only the first two have curves."*

## THREAD 8 — Central banking after labor: the Tap, the glut, and the Dividend Dollar

**The legal revolution.** The **People's Tap** began as emergency policy (2037, Labor-Debt Crunch): direct Fed transfers to household accounts under a stretched 13(3). *United States v. Whitmore* (2039, 4–4–1) upheld the authority; the Tap was wound down 2042–44, reopened as the 2041–45 financing bridge ($4.9T cumulative, published amortization rule — 06 §5), and its institutional residue is the **FedNow-Card**: the Treasury/Fed direct-account rail on which the Copper Card runs. Deposit banking died of it: with r* negative and the Tap paying into direct accounts, retail deposits fled the commercial banks by 2041 — the banks' response (fleet-leasing portfolio reorientation, the co-op banks' rise) is the financial history's other half.

**The glut and the rates.** Fleet profits pooled as savings faster than the dividend spent them down (owners' marginal propensity to consume < recipients'), producing a **savings tsunami**: r* ≈ −0.5% through the 2040s, recovering toward +0.2% only in the 2050s as dividend diffusion raised consumption. The Fed adopted **NGDP-level targeting** (2044, the "Marsh Compromise": the Fed targets the level path of nominal demand; Treasury manages distribution) — the regime that let nominal GDP sag while GWO compounded without a debt-deflation spiral. The 2051 BIS retrospective's formulation: *monetary policy in the fleet economy is demand scheduling, and fiscal policy is demand creation; the old assignment (money for prices, fiscal for allocation) inverted.*

**Sovereign debt and the fiscal inversion.** The **IMF Fiscal Inversion Framework** (2042): the manual for converting payroll-tax states into machine-tax states (the Four-Legged Stool's export kit); advanced-economy debt/GDP peaked ~130% (2046) and fell thereafter — growth plus negative-rates plus the fleet levies' widening base. The framework's famous annex table lists the precondition that predicted national fates (01 §8's five): *the tax system must move before the base dies* — Norway (14 months) vs Iraq (never).

**Currencies.** Three-way, settling by Y20. The **e-CNY** became the SCO-C's settlement rail — fleet-hours invoiced in e-CNY within the bloc, 22% of cross-border settlement by Y20, its real innovation the *machine-readable invoice* (fleet-hours as a unit of account — the first currency whose unit is labor). The euro became the Grid Union's **electron-currency** (HVDC contracts settle in EUR-denominated MWh; the ECB's "kWh corridor" is monetary policy for electrons). The dollar stayed the reserve — the **"Dividend Dollar"** thesis: the Copper Card rail (300M+ accounts, the deepest consumer payment network ever), Treasury depth, and the Hong Kong Fleet Convention's SDR-fleet-unit (the **robot SDR**: fleet-hours as a reserve asset priced in dollars) kept the core, while the *edges* (SCO-C settlement, Grid Union electrons, NACM's clearing) pluralized. The BIS's verdict, quoted in 01: the dollar won the transition the way it won 1944 — by being where the invoices were when the new economy signed them.

**The Meridian aftermath.** The Meridian Rule (2042) — model-fiduciary duties on autonomous financial agents — plus the Basel-M framework (2043: capital charges on model-driven books, the "model risk weight") are the financial system's HE Amendment: machines may trade, but a human owns the outcome. Meridian-7's $2.1T remains the cost of the lesson; the 2044–49 autonomous-fund industry that rebuilt under the Rule has, notably, never produced a second Meridian — the sim's quiet testimony that one law, written in eight months, can hold for a generation.

## THREAD 9 — The post-labor city

**The demand flip.** A city in 2026 sells proximity to jobs. A city in 2054 sells proximity to *people* — the consumption-density machine (05 §3), with urban form rebuilt around the three flows abundance made valuable: contact (the Care Districts and the Contact Commons), craft (the guild quarters), and governance (the agora buildings).

**The Care District.** Every adapter city's human-premium quarter: clinics, guild halls, teaching kitchens, the certified-care blocks, the human-noticed schools, zoned (by the "presence codes" of 2046–52) near transit and parks and — the land-economics note — *near hospitals*, because the land boom's reversal (errata 7) hit speculative districts first while care-anchored districts held. The Contact Commons are the form's public half: roofed streets, bathhouse-grade civic interiors, the "third place" industrialized — the Association Dividend's real estate program, and the reason adapter-city library visits tripled while book circulation fell.

**Detroit.** The sim's exhibit city. Robot manufacturing capital of the US ("Robot Valley, Michigan": Kinetiq's Downriver Plant X, OmniFleet's Riverworks refit yards, the supplier belt across Windsor), first US metro to grow post-Crossing (+9% population Y5–Y15), governed through the crisis years by Mayor **Renata Cole** — negotiator of the Detroit Protocols (06 §2) — elected three times on the platform that made her famous: *"The machines were built here. The rules get written here."* The Protocols' negotiated genesis in a city that had lost everything to the last automation and gained everything from this one is the corpus's neatest symbol-structure; the Detroit Historical Museum's Transition Hall (opened 2051) is the most-visited museum of the era.

**Office towers.** The Convert-or-Collapse wave (2039–45): CBD office occupancy 52% → 78% by Y15 via conversion — residential floors, vertical farms, guild halls, care units; the cities that subsidized conversion (NYC's 2041 program, Toronto, Madrid) kept their tax bases; the ones that litigated it (the landlords' "reversion suits") spent a decade in court and lost the decade. The skyscraper's cultural demotion (04 §2) has its fiscal twin: the CBD premium inverted, and the monocore city became the polynucleated one — the "constellation city," twelve mid-density centers on transit spines, which is the urban plan every 2050s masterplan contains.

**Retail.** The boutique renaissance: human-premium service retail (the certified barber, the tasting counter, the repair guild) occupying the ruins of the chains the robots' prices had already killed; logistics micro-hubs (the last-mile fleets) occupying the rest. The mall, of all things, returned — as the Contact Commons' suburban form.

**The Rooted, designed for** (per 05's urbanist dissent): the "village upgrade" programs — fiber, care-robot pods, the minibus fleets, the guild outposts — applied *in place*, on the 80-km principle: most people stayed where the wages died, and the smartest policy of the 2040s was the one that treated that as the plan rather than the failure. The Heartland Commons and the Monastery Belt campuses are the program's rural bookends; the polynucleated constellation city is its urban twin; and the true city of Y30 contains both, because the Great Sorting was always a minority event.

## THREAD 10 — The care economy: the sector that never crossed

**The thesis, tested.** Canon: caregiving is the never-fully-automated human-premium sector. The Kaigo-Robo program's task decomposition (Japan, national data, Y9): **lifting, hygiene, feeding, monitoring, overnight watch — 60% automated**; the residue — what the program's own report called *kizuki* ("noticing") — **0% automated, by design**: the Ministry's certification standard requires a human to hold the noticing function for any licensed care bed. The HE Amendment's Contribution clause (06 §4) and the EU's care directives enshrine the same asymmetry: machines may carry; humans must *notice*.

**The premium, quantified.** US certified-caregiver loaded wage: $38/hr (Y0) → **$41/hr (Y15)** — the only large occupational category whose real wage *rose* through the transition (the Human-Contact Premium, 03 §6, at its clearest). The elder-care month: $4,200 (Y0, robot-light) → $6,800 (Y15, the human-contact tier) — a *price increase* in a deflating economy, the era's most counterintuitive price series, and the direct consequence of scarcity relocating from stuff to persons. The premium is broad: certified-human therapy (+60%), hospitality (+40%), early-childhood (+55%), thanatology and hospice (+70% — the era's fastest-growing licensed profession).

**The workforce.** Y0 global care workforce ~120M (formal + informal). The transition's care arithmetic: demand *rises* (aging: median ages per 05 §4; the Healthspan Dividend adds healthy years that are, precisely, care-consuming years) while supply's old subsidy — women's unpaid labor and migrants' remittance-seeking — is re-priced by the dividend. The gap closed by: the **Care Corps** (US 2046: 600k → 2.2M by Y20; the Civic Corps' care rotation as national service-in-all-but-name), the UK's Purpose Prescribing care rotations, the EU's care co-ops (the Techniker Trust model applied to care: the **Milan Carello** federation, 214 co-ops by Y15), and the **Care Visas** (05 §2): 4.1M certified human-contact migrants by Y20 — the Grey Migration's labor twin, and the reversal of the migration century: the North importing presence.

**The crèche finding.** Phase 5's scoreboard made childcare-robots the pro-natal winner (+0.15–0.25 TFR, compounding); Thread 10's addition is the *labor* finding: crèche-robots did not reduce care employment — they increased it (the margaret does the lifting; the certified human does the noticing; the ratio of humans-to-children in licensed crèches *rose* 0.9 → 1.4 per 8 children as the task mix shifted entirely to contact). The sim's cleanest policy sentence, from the Kaigo-Robo commission's final report: **"Automation did not shrink the care economy. It stripped it down to its irreducible core, and paid the core better."**

**The Sokos boundary.** The care file's hardest case: synthetic companionship as care. The Copenhagen Convention (2047) and the Human-in-the-Room acts draw the line — adjunct, not substitute — and the market confirms it: Sokos-tier companionship is a $90B industry by Y20, and the human-contact tier *above* it is $210B and growing faster. The finding the 2064 WAS data supports: synthetic intimacy relieves loneliness's *floor* and cannot supply its *standing* — you cannot be relied upon by a model, and being relied upon, per the whole Slack Line file, was the thing the century's people were starving for.
