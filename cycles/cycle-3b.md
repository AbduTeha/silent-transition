## THREAD 26 — Ghost fleets and the Botmafia: the political economy of the dark fleet

**The size question, settled as a range.** Official: 0.5% of installed base (~5.5M units, Y15). Calloway's energy-residual estimate: **3% (~33M units)** — adopted by 08 §11 as the honest upper band. The 2051 Geneva Group audit split the difference with a decomposition the field now uses: **1.4% true ghosts** (built in ghost factories or slipped off-ledger), **0.9% "gray conversions"** (legitimate units re-tasked off-ledger — the Botmafia's rental market), **0.7% "orphaned stock"** (decommissioned units never deregistered, still running: the fleet economy's unmarked graves, concentrated in collapsed-transition states). Illicit robot-economy revenue: **$180–300B/yr at Y15** — between the official and dissent figures, still dwarfed by the $4.8T legal fleet-hour economy it parasitizes.

**The supply chain.** Ghost factories are the Prohibition stills of the fleet age: small, distributed, permanently ahead of inspection. The majors: the **Camorra's Naples line** (frame-and-actuator assembly from gray-market Chinese Tier-2 parts, ~400k units/yr capacity at peak); the **'Ndranghta's siting network** (ghost factories under legitimate logistics parks — the 2048 Parma raid found 6,000 units assembling inside a cheese warehouse's cold chain); the **Yakuza ledger houses** (maintenance and false-registry services — the "white plate" industry: stolen legitimate identities for gray units); the **PCC's southern trade** (São Paulo as the hemisphere's ghost hub, the Paraguay free zone its Singapore); and the **Fleet Crews** of the collapsed states (Karachi, Khartoum, Dhaka-adjacent), whose innovation was not technical but organizational: the co-op model, criminalized — member-owned ghost fleets, dividends in kind, dispute resolution by the elders who once ran the katchi abadis' water committees.

**The demand side is the scandal.** The Botmafia's customer base is not criminal; it is *priced out*: the unlicensed care bed (a night of gray care at $12 vs $85 certified — the gray care market is 6% of OECD care hours per the 2053 Mutual audit, concentrated in the hours the certified system rations), the off-books haulage, the cheap renovation crew, the domestic unit without the subscription. The enforcement dilemma the Detroit generation never faced: every ghost fleet seized makes some poor household's care more expensive. The truce the era reached — **"attest the base, tolerate the margin"** (the 2050 Naples Doctrine: inspectorates target factories and registries, not household gray units; amnesty-and-attest programs legalized 2.1M units in 2051–53 at $90/attestation) — cut the ghost base's *growth* rate to near zero while leaving the stock. The holdouts: the combat-adjacent gray market (unattested swarm risk — the Yemen lesson's dark twin), which the pools price out and the drones hunt.

**The forensic arms race.** The grid heartbeat (Calloway's method — energy residuals as census) is now institutionalized: the **Load Watch** division of the International Energy Agency's successor publishes annual unexplained-draw maps, the era's most-cited crime statistics. The arms race is otherwise symmetrical and unending: attestation layers versus spoofing, the Zurich-II seals versus the Bath-grade forgers, model fingerprinting versus laundering. The corpus's verdict, from the 2059 UNODC review: *the dark fleet is not a law-enforcement problem with a solution; it is a shadow of the legitimate economy that grows and shrinks with the legitimate economy's generosity to the priced-out — the first crime wave in history that welfare policy directly polices.*

## THREAD 27 — The Translation Flat: languages after machines

**The event.** Real-time machine translation crossed conversational adequacy in 2036 and *indistinguishability* by 2040 (the fleet-era earbud generation — the "Flat" — named for what it did to language's hills). The measured sequence: language-learning industry −80% by Y12 (the Duolingo-class firms' pivot to heritage archives); subtitle-free travel's mass adoption by 2039; the **diplomatic ear** (machine-mediated negotiation with attested nuance logs) standard by 2043; and the quiet datum that defines the era: **by Y15, a majority of cross-lingual conversations on Earth are machine-mediated** — the first time in the species' history that most people, most of the time, speak to strangers through a machine.

**What survived, what flourished.** The Flat's predicted casualty — small languages — instead *revived*, for three reasons the sociolinguists still argue about: (1) **the corpus dividend**: the preservation programs (the **2,000-Language Corpus**, 2041, UNESCO-Microsoft-descendant consortium; community compensation for speech data — the "voice royalties," $1.4B/yr flowing to speaker communities by Y20) made every language *trainable*, which made every language *serviceable*, which removed the economic argument for shift; (2) **the literature effect**: with translation free, reading *in* the original became a premium act — the "untranslated renaissance" (small-language fiction sales tripled in translation-era markets: the Icelandic, Wolof, Māori, Santali lists are the era's prestige imprints); (3) **identity in abundance**: when everyone could speak everything, the one thing a language still signaled was *belonging* — the Flat made language choice a pure statement of affiliation for the first time in history, and affiliation, the corpus has established, is what people do with freedom.

**What actually declined:** English *as necessity* — the lingua-franca function automated away; English-as-second-language instruction collapsed, English *as culture* held (the Anglosphere's entertainment share fell only 8 points), and the foreign ministries' interpreters became the last profession to be automated-then-rehumanized (the **attested interpreter** — the human whose presence certifies the diplomatic record — is the Audit State's smallest and most romantic guild). The second casualty: **the monolingual mind**. The generation raised in the Flat reads one language and speaks through the machine — the "single-language, all-languages" cohort — and the educational response (the **Rooted Language curriculum**: every child certifies in one heritage language *plus* the machine) is the 2050s' pedagogical compromise.

**The politics.** The Flat's governance fights were about *voice sovereignty*: the 2044 **Francophonie Protocols** (cultural products machine-translated from French must carry the "prix-fixe" attestation of the source community), the 2047 **Māori Television settlement** (the template for indigenous data sovereignty over voice models), and the running controversy the corpus records without resolving — the **"one-voice problem"**: the models' normative accent (the training corpora's center of gravity) quietly homogenizing world speech, measurable as a 6% decline in phonological diversity across 40 languages, 2040–2058 — the Flat's cost, paid in a currency nobody priced until it had fallen.

## THREAD 28 — The last professions: the internal politics of the 5–9%

**The roster at Y30.** Employment in adapter nations concentrates in: oversight/audit (judges, attestors, notaries, election officers — ~5% of adults in certified roles, part-time included), human-premium care and contact (caregivers, teachers at 1:7, therapists, hospitality, the porter's guilds), craft (the guild system, Handmark chains), civic (Civic Corps rotations, sortition service), red-key military ethicists and maintainers, and the attention/craft economy's earning tier. Median certified-worker wage: $41–52/hr loaded; hours: the **20-hour standard** (the 2049 ILO convention) — the last professions work half-time at double pay, by design: the premium is priced against *presence per hour*, and presence, like any luxury, is rationed.

**The guild confederation.** The last professions organized fast and late: the **Licensed Humans** confederation (2047, from the care unions and the notaries' colleges) — 21M members by Y20 — whose program is the era's labor-politics archive: certification boards (capture-resistant by sortition-selected lay seats), the premium's *fidelity* (anti-"attestation theater" enforcement — the guilds' own inspectorates, since the Karnataka Registry's 2057 speech made drift respectable to name), and the **obligation culture**: the working minority's norm, formalized in the HE Amendment's Contribution clause jurisprudence, that certified presence carries a service duty (pro bono hours — 8/month standard) because the premium is society's subsidy wearing wages.

**The resentments, both directions.** The **"Takers" slur** (the working minority on the dividend majority — virulent 2040s, fading as the index diffusion matured) and its mirror, the **"Rentiers"** (the majority on the certified minority's premiums — the 2053 "Care Strike" wave, when certified caregivers in six countries struck for premium floors and won: the only successful general strikes of the era, tolerated by governments that had learned who actually holds the society's scarce factor). The settlement that followed — the **Premium Accords** (2054: certified professions accept published rate cards and service duties; states accept the premium as untaxable income up to 1.5× median) — is the last professions' Magna Carta and the tax system's quiet exception.

**The gatekeeping problem.** Every corpus file that touches certification eventually touches its drift (Priya's attestation theater; the Hand Checks; the ghost interpreters). The last professions' internal politics is therefore, at bottom, the Authenticity Turn run as labor relations: the guilds policing their own marks, the sortition chambers auditing the auditors (the Yaw Chambers' first subpoena, 2064, was the notaries' college — a constitutional rite of passage), and the standing finding of the 2063 ILO review: *the last jobs are the best jobs in history, and their greatest risk is that they become exactly what the old economy was — a ladder pulled up behind the people who climbed it.* The Contribution clause's open-admission programs (the "second-chair" rights: every certified role reserves seats for first-generation entrants) are the counter-force, and their enrollment data is the sim's quiet measure of whether the fleet century's working class stays open or congeals.

## THREAD 29 — Ocean and orbital frontiers: the exit-and-build economy

**The land-rent break, mechanically stated.** Errata 7's partial land reversal (post-Y18) has three causes, in order of magnitude: (1) **the Idle Fleet made location cheap** — production follows energy and materials, not people; (2) **ocean platforms** moved the heavy, dirty, and boring offshore (the flag registries' competition drove platform permitting costs to 1/8 of coastal equivalents); (3) **orbital industry** took the two workloads geography could not serve (heat rejection and vacuum). The measured result: prime-coastal and superstar-metro land −22% real from the Y8 peak by Y30 — a *softening*, not a collapse — while the frontier asset classes (orbital licenses, platform berths, the Grid Union's desert arrays) absorbed the rents the cities surrendered. The Georgists were right that rents pool; wrong (again) about where.

**The ocean decade.** The **Seastead Decade (2046–56)** delivered, at industrial scale and residential nano-scale: the **Azores Freeport** (Portugal's 2047 charter: 214 platforms by Y30 — aquaculture fleets, desal-barge clusters, the Atlantic data-center ring cooled by the Gulf Stream), Singapore's **Tuas Extension** (the sovereign platform-city, 90,000 residents by Y30 — the one genuine urban success, because it had a city's law), the PIF's **Red Sea Rings** (tourism and marine research, gorgeous and half-empty), and the failure class the genre predicted: the charter-city startups of the accelerationist right (nine launched, one solvent — *Exit* was easier than *Build*, and the ones that worked had, without exception, signed on to someone's existing legal order: the sea's lesson is that jurisdiction is the scarce input, not steel).

**The orbital economy.** Launch $190/kg (Y12) → $95 (Y20) → $40 (Y30, the reusable-heavy plateau). The stack: orbital compute (8 GW Y20 → 30 GW Y30, canon), the **exocloud** (fleet inference in transit), orbital solar's firming contracts (the Sunbelt Sovereigns' second act), the pharmacology cluster (2057), and the beginning of serious mass: ~11,000 people living off-Earth at Y30 (stations, not settlements; the "town" is still the ship). The **Outer Registry** logs ~4,000 platform-hours daily; the **2061 labor dispute** (the Fenghuang maintenance fleet's strike over rotation schedules — arbitrated under Outer Registry rules, settled with the off-world 20-hour standard) is technically the frontier's first industrial-relations precedent and, the labor historians note, proof that wherever the fleet economy goes, the guilds follow within the decade.

**The dividend question.** Who owns the frontier? The pattern so far repeats the terrestrial one, compressed: state-backed consortia and the Sunbelt funds own the orbital layer; the **Orbital Commons proposal** (2059, the Non-Aligned bloc's demand for a per-capita share of orbital spectrum and orbit-slot rents — the "orbit dividend") is the Y30 era's unfinished treaty fight, and the corpus's closing observation is the Long Walk's mirror: *every frontier in this century has been claimed before its abundance arrived, and the claims are always contested by the people the last frontier skipped.*

## THREAD 30 — Standards diplomacy: the attestation stack's world government

**The structure.** By Y30 the world runs on two convergent capability stacks and a referee layer: the **Pact stack** (the Singapore Compact's attestation lineage — weights-provenance, firmware attestation, the Zurich craft chain, the Fleet Protocol's interoperability layer) and the **Zhuangzhou stack** (CGRG's parallel realm), with the **Joint Attestation Commission (JAC, 2050)** — hosted in Taipei, chartered by both — certifying cross-stack equivalence: the world's quietest treaty organization, 340 staff, and more practical authority over daily life than the Security Council.

**The referee's economics.** Taiwan's attestation services (the verification-neutrality role, 02 §3): **$14B/yr receipts by Y20**, the island's second export after silicon-adjacent services — neutrality as an industry, with the delicate politics its neutrality requires: the JAC's charter (the "Taipei Rules") prohibits the referee from *ranking* the stacks, only from *mapping* them — and the mapping fights are the century's real diplomacy (the 2049 kill-switch standard: eleven months, two near-walkouts, resolved by the "dual-attestation envelope" that lets each stack implement the safety requirement in its own idiom — the compromise that became the JAC's method).

**The case files.** The standards wars, three specimens: (1) the **kill-switch standard (2049)** — post-Shanghai-Water, everyone wanted a master interrupt; the fight was over *who holds the hand* (resolved: the owner's device, the insurer's bond, the state's warrant — three keys, no single custodian, the HE Amendment's logic in hardware); (2) the **care-noticing standard (2053)** — what fraction of a care hour must be human-attended for the certification (the " noticing floor": 15% of licensed hours, human, on-site — the care economy's constitutional clause, written in a standards committee over the objection of every efficiency study and to the gratitude of every subsequent survey); (3) the **autonomy taxonomy (2056)** — the Seoul Regime's successor classification (Tool/Agent/Proxy/Emergent), which the Estonian Trilemma anchored and the "Emergent" category (systems that modify their own goals beyond attestation) keeps threatening to escape.

**The method.** The era substituted standards for treaties — faster, technical, self-enforcing (compliance is priced by the pools, Thread 22) — and the substitution is why the century's international relations are, in the JAC's own phrase, *"a correspondence between committee reports."* The Trade in Machine Services round (2052, the WTO's successor instrument) wrote the trade law of the fleet economy in nine months because its drafts were standards documents, not treaty texts. The comparativists' summary, which the corpus adopts as this thread's verdict: *the Peace of Westphalia took a century of war to write down; the Peace of Taipei took eleven months of committee time, and it holds — because it does not ask who rules, only whose arithmetic the other side can audit.*
