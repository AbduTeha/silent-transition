## THREAD 11 — Education after jobs: from K-12 to K-Care

**The collapse.** The sector's organizing fiction — education as human-capital formation for employment — died with the Junior Freeze, and the numbers tracked it: US university enrollment fell **70% from the 2025 peak to 2044**; 340 four-year institutions closed or merged Y5–Y15; the law schools (53,000 JDs/yr at peak) graduated 4,100 in 2046; the MBA rebranded or died. But the sector did not shrink — it *metamorphosed*, because the one function enrollment data had always hidden was the one the Slack Week made priceless: **custody**. School is where the children are while the adults are alive to themselves. The 2042 OECD review said it plainly: "The industrial school was a babysitting service with a curriculum attached. The curriculum has been canceled. The service remains."

**K-Care.** The replacement architecture, standard across adapter nations by Y12: (1) **Foundation years** (4–12): the crèche-robot model — margarets do the lifting, feeding, and transport; certified humans (the 1.4-per-8 ratios of Cycle 1, Thread 10) do the noticing; curriculum = literacy, numeracy, *and* the "management of a free week" (the timetable skills the Slack Week had destroyed). (2) **Guild years** (12–18): the Apprenticeship Act (US 2048; EU 2047; Japan 2046) guarantees every adolescent **two days a week in a guild placement** — craft, care, civic, stage, field — under the Handmark chain-of-custody so that a teenager's work is *documented as theirs*; the portfolio, not the diploma, is the credential. (3) **The folkehøjskole layer**: the Danish folk-high-school revival (04 §4's winner) went global — residential, non-credential, year-long "formation" terms that the five-phase life enters and re-enters; by Y30 one adult in six in adapter nations has attended one, at median age 31.

**AI tutors and the Tutor Wars.** The models were perfect tutors by 2030 and the politics were 2030's too: the **Tutor Stack** (the Public Model Trust's education layer, free, attested) versus the commercial stacks (Pearson-descendant "Meridian Learning," the Korean hagwon giants' "Sugye" system) — and the finding that decided the decade: **tutoring was the easy 80%; the supervised struggle was the irreplaceable 20%**. Cohorts with full AI tutoring and no cohort structure learned faster and *wanted* less (the Goalless pattern, age 14); the adapter settlements therefore funded the *cohort* (the guild years, the teams, the choirs) and let the tutoring be free. The 2049 evaluation ("the Helsinki replication") is education research's most-cited result: attainment up, ambition neutral-to-down without structure — Jahoda for children.

**Who teaches.** The certified-human teacher became the premium profession (the $41/hr caregiver's cousin): teaching rolls *grew* 11% Y10–Y25 in adapter nations even as enrollment collapsed — the ratio inverted from 1:16 to 1:7 — and the profession's status re-rated (03 §4's standing markets, local-politics branch). The university survived as three things: the research machine (Thread 12), the guild schools' degree-granting annexes, and the **Senior Colleges** — the Lucky Retirees' enrollment boom being the sector's only growth line by Y20 (the "third-age degree," 4.1M enrolled US 2054).

**The honest ledger.** The transition's education outcome is the corpus's most double-edged: the best-served cohorts in human history (the Digital Natives: tutor-rich, guild-documented, custody-generous) *and* the worst-served (the Junior Freeze generation, who aged out of every institution mid-restructure — 03's scar tissue, never offered the guild years because the guilds were built after they left school). The 2061 UNESCO review's verdict: *"The fleet century built a magnificent school system one cohort too late, and has been apologizing to that cohort ever since."*

## THREAD 12 — The discovery machine: science, longevity, fusion, and who owns what's found

**The Self-Driving Lab network.** By Y8 the frontier of experimental science was robotic: the **SDL network** (Berkeley's LBNL hub, the Cambridge-Max Planck ring, Shenzhen's CAS line, Hyderabad's CSIR cluster) — hypothesis generation by model, experiment by robot, 10,000 materials syntheses/day at the peak nodes. The productivity numbers are the era's cleanest: cost per discovered material fell 400× Y0–Y15; battery chemistry (solid-state electrolytes, the sodium-cathode family that reshaped African mineral demand), catalysts (green ammonia at $180/ton by 2044 — the Grid Union's fertilizer base), and pharmaceuticals (the Phase-II success rate 12% → 38% by Y12) were the headline sectors. The constraint was never ideas; it was **validation** — and the SDL era's bottleneck science became exactly the residue the whole corpus predicts: peer validation, replication, and the *witnessing* of evidence became the human-premium professions of the lab (the "attested experiment": a robot-run result without a human witness chain is, per the 2047 Zurich-II extension, inadmissible for regulatory use).

**Longevity.** The pipeline: the TAME-successor trials (2038–46, the geroprotector combinations) delivered the first real cohort effects; the **Healthspan Dividend** (canon, 2052+) is the actuarial confirmation — median healthspan +4 years within a decade of deployment, driven by three arms: the GLP-descendant metabolic class, the senolytic combinations, and — the underrated arm — care-robot mobility (falls are the old age's assassination, and margarets abolished the fall). Cost: the combination therapies priced at $2,900/yr by 2054 (the SDL pipeline's deflation), covered everywhere by the settlements (UCA, UCA-equivalents). The fiscal paradox the actuaries flagged and the dividend math absorbed: longer healthy lives *cost the settlements nothing* because nothing in the settlement's arithmetic priced years of life — the pension problem had died with wages. The equity question — the 2040s' "longevity gap" (adapter-nation access vs the corridor states) — closed slower than the science: the 2058 Lagos Mutual longevity pool is the AFC's answer, arriving as always a decade late and then permanently.

**Fusion.** The Mirage (2048–50) — two announced breakthroughs, one retracted, one real-but-late — ended with the canon landmarks: **Hefei CFR-Next, first grid-parity baseload, 2051**; the US Livermore-Iter consortium, 2053. Y30 fusion supplies ~6% of world electricity (concentrated in China, the US, the Grid Union's baseload ring) — the revolution that mattered less than advertised because solar-plus-storage had already won the price war ($0.012/kWh wholesale at Y20, canon): fusion's role was *firming*, not generation. The sim's energy footnote, for the record: the fleet century's power system is 61% solar+wind+storage at Y30, 18% fission (the SMR fleets of the 2040s), 6% fusion, 15% hydro/geo/other — and the Sunbelt Sovereigns' second act (selling firm capacity and compute cycles) is built on exactly this mix.

**Ownership: the Discovery Patents Act (2049).** The question the SDL era forced: who owns what the machines find? The US answer, copied in the NACM states and half the EU: **five-year exclusivity, then public domain** — model-discovered art gets a short, non-renewable window (enough to fund the trial), then the commons. The Act's floor statement (Sen. Amara Diallo, D-CO) is the era's IP sentence: *"Discovery was never the scarce thing. Validation was. We pay for the checking, not for the finding."* The pharmaceutical majors' response — pivoting to validation networks and manufacturing — is why, at Y30, the largest holders of patent *portfolios* are insurance pools and sovereign funds, not laboratories: the last irony of the ownership question, filed where nobody expected it.

## THREAD 13 — The home robot product cadence: Margaret v1–v6

**The line.** Ampersand's Margaret (named, the founders admitted, for the most common name of the last human generation to raise children without robots) is the transition's iPhone — the product that made the fleet economy *domestic*:

| Model | Year | Price/lease | The capability that sold it | Installed base (cum.) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| v1 "Margaret" | 2036 | $19k / $390/mo | Laundry-to-drawer; kitchen reset; the 40-task core | 1.9M |
| v2 | 2038 | $14k / $290/mo | Child-sensing (the Margaret Rules, below); elder-watch | 9M |
| v3 | 2041 | $9k / $199/mo | The "Margaret Home" subscription: robot + care tier + errand fleet | 41M |
| v4 | 2046 | $6k / $149/mo | Full grocery/cook chain; the "second pair of hands" latency fix | 130M |
| v5 | 2052 | $4k / $99/mo | Whole-home fleet orchestration (the home as micro-fleet) | 310M |
| v6 | 2059 | $2.8k / $79/mo | The companion-tactile tier (Copenhagen-compliant) | 520M |

2038 is the home economy's Year Zero — the **Year of the Home Robot** (v2's child-sensing crossed the trust threshold; installed base decupled in four years). The **Margaret Rules** (2038, the US CPSC standard adopted globally by 2040) are the household fleet's Geneva Protocol: mandatory registry identity, the "human hand rule" for children (a robot may block a toddler's path; only a human may restrain), no-child-alone certification, and the kill-switch right — the household's version of the Detroit settlement, written by the same logic and, notably, by some of the same lawyers.

**Adoption, by decile.** The diffusion was wealthier-first and slower than the phone's (the Squeeze's deflation was real but households' incomes were in transition): top-decile saturation 2039; median-household adoption 2043 (the crossover the AMP board called "the day the platform became furniture"); bottom-quintile 2048 — via the settlements' device inclusion (the Copper Card's household supplement in the US, the EU care packages). Gender is the finding the era did not foresee predicting and the data made undeniable: the household robot fell on the *second shift* (04's inheritance), so its adoption raised **women's leisure hours by 7.1/week and men's by 2.3** — the gender gap in domestic labor *widened* in hours-terms for the first time since 1965, because the robot took the tasks each partner most resented... and the tasks men most resented were the ones women had been doing. The 2049 *Journal of Family Studies* symposium ("Margaret's Inheritance") is the transition's most reprinted sociology.

**The second-order markets.** The **Margaret Economies**: the errand-fleet integration (v3's subscription made the household a node in the urban fleet web — your robot books the delivery metal, the shares the garage dock); the "home fleet insurance" rider (TIAA-Prime's largest Y15 product line); the retrofit wave (the "Margaret-ready" home certificate, 2044, the era's renovation standard); and the afterlife — Stillstandsnacht's domestic wing: retired household units decommissioned with the same ceremonies as the industrial fleets (04 §5.4), because, as the Zwickau liturgy holds, *they carried what we could not*.

**The failure.** For the record: the **sex-robot decade** (2039–47) commercially under-delivered — the tactile tier's adoption plateaued at 3% of households, against 22% for the conversational Sokos tier (canon) — the market's own verdict that the scarce thing was *attention*, not simulation. The Copenhagen Convention's intimacy-log rules were written for the conversation tier; the tactile tier got regulated like appliances and culturally like embarrassment, and the corpus records the asymmetry without resolving it.

## THREAD 14 — Logistics end-to-end: the death and rebirth of the movement of things

**The trucking wave.** 3.5M US heavy-truck drivers at Y0 — the largest male-majority occupation in the country, and the Squeeze's cruelest sequence: long-haul autonomy arrived (Y1–2) *before* the fleet economy's make-work, so the displacement hit the exact cohort (48-year-old ex-mill workers with CDLs) the twenty-first century had already failed once. The **I-80 Blockades** (Oct 2037–Feb 2038): eleven states, 40,000 trucks, the Wyoming closure that food prices made famous; ended not by force but by the **Trucker Bridge** (Okafor's 2038 program: 5-year wage insurance at 90%, CDL-equivalent contribution credit toward the coming dividend, and — the clause the drivers' spokescouncil, the *Rolling Brotherhood*, actually demanded — a fleet-equity option: truckers could take 30% of their bridge as shares in the automated fleets). The Bridge is Distributionism's first statutory win (04 §6's genealogy starts here, not with Batista's pamphlet), and its alumni sit, to this day, on the co-op boards of the freight fleets that replaced them.

**Ports.** Fully automated by 2040 (the International Longshoremen's 2036 settlement — the **Automation Dividend clauses**: every displaced container job funded 1.5 fleet shares + pension enhancement + the "gang precedent" preserved as the port co-ops' board seats). Container shipping itself: overcapacity hell (the labor content of trade collapsed; demand for *movement* grew but *rates* fell 60% by Y10; the mega-carrier consolidation of 2041–43, four alliances → one, ended by the same logic as the Fleet Protocol — the Hong Kong Fleet Convention's maritime annex).

**The Freight Commons.** The urban layer: municipal last-mile fleets (the "commons trucks" — dispatch pooled, open-protocol, the Fleet Protocol's best-loved application), the micro-hub network (Thread 9), and the **night freight covenant** (2043, the cities' answer to curb chaos: heavy urban movement restricted to 23:00–05:00, when the streets are empty and the robots' low noise profile makes the city's night logistics silent — the "sound of the transition," the era's writers called it: a city humming at a frequency humans feel as calm).

**Rail's renaissance.** The sleeper hit of the logistics file: with labor free and energy cheap, *steel wheels* beat rubber everywhere above 300 km — the Robo-Rail decade (2042–52) doubled world rail freight, electrified the trunk lines, and revived the passenger networks (the night-train renaissance is cultural canon: the Grid Union's Trans-European sleeper web, 2047, is the era's most beloved infrastructure and its most photographed sunset).

**The flops, honestly filed.** Air taxis (the 2020s' promised decade): regulated into niche by the insurance pools (the actuarial verdict on 10,000 consumer aircraft over cities was 'no'); the Hyperloop-class proposals (never broke the wall between demo and grid); and — the file's irony — **the last-mile human**: by Y20, the courier-with-a-cart is a licensed human-premium profession in every rich city (the "porter's guild," Handmark-adjacent), because the doorstep is a *presence* moment and presence, as ever, is the residue the fleet economy cannot synthesize. The transition killed the driver and hired the porter; the freight file's whole economics is that sentence.

## THREAD 15 — Sex, family, and the companion economy: the demography of intimacy

**The Sokos economy, mapped.** Companion-AI prevalence (canon: 22% of unmarried adults name an AI primary companion by Y12) stratifies by the corpus's now-standard variables: highest among the Slack Week's longest-tenured (the 2046 cohort data), the mobility-limited, and the recently bereaved; lowest among the cohort-rich (guild members, congregation members — the Third Structures are also the human-contact supply). Revenue: $38B (Y10) → $90B (Y20) → $130B (Y30), with the margin concentrated in the **grief-adjacent tier** (testamentary-consent memorials, the Copenhagen-regulated class) and the **rehearsal tier** (social-skills training for the contact-shy — the tier the therapists endorse). The standing question — *does the floor become a ceiling?* — has its answer in the 2056 Helsinki longitudinal: 62% of Sokos-primary users formed human partnerships within six years; 14% are the "quiet rooms" (the ceiling, real and irreversible for its members); 24% cycled — and the intervention that moved the number was not regulation but **structure**: users enrolled in any Third Structure showed human-partnership rates indistinguishable from never-users. The Slack Line, again, wearing cologne.

**Marriage and household formation.** The bifurcation (03 §6) with numbers: the **companionship-marriage** (median age at first marriage rose to 33; the "vow economy" — weddings are the era's only *growing* ceremonial sector, +140% real Y0–Y20, because a vow is the one ritual that must be performed in front of bodies) versus the despair-decoupling wave (Y2–Y7, divorce +38% at peak, then a plateau the field reads as *completion*: the marriages that would fail, failed early). Divorce rate at Y30: −40% vs Y0. Household composition: the **multigenerational return** (three-generation households 12% → 24% of adapter-nation totals — the Gray Dividend's housing expression: care robots made the grandmother's room an amenity, not a burden), the **kinship clubs** (the poly-network and chosen-family charters, legal personality in the Netherlands 2048, Estonia's Proxy category as their e-personhood vehicle), and the single-person household *plateauing and falling after 2049* — the first decline in the series since the census began, driven by (in order) multigenerational return, companionship-marriage, and the quiet rooms' residents being counted, for the first time, as households-of-two.

**Ectogenesis: the Womb Politics decade.** France's **Ectogenesis Act (2051)**: licensed, capped, and ritually restricted — the Maison Lumière (Lyon, 2052) delivered the first cohort (214 children); the Act's design is the era's bioethics classic: a national cap (5,000/yr), a licensed-parent regime, a **contact covenant** (the birth record's human-witness chain; the child's right to the attested circumstances of their beginning), and the Church-shaped carve-out (the Vatican's 2052 rescript: ectogenetic birth is valid baptism; the soul does not check the plumbing — the sentence that ended a century's argument in one clause). US state-by-state adoption 2053–57; the federal fights (the "Womb Wars," 2054–58) ended in the Compromise of 2058: no federal ban, no federal funding, HE-Amendment protections for ectogenetic children (the "Newborn Equality" clauses). Demography, as Phase 5 scored it: +0.02 TFR nationally by Y30 — small; culturally enormous; and the pipeline's real yield may arrive after the sim's horizon (the 2062 Geneva Group forecast: ectogenesis+crèche-robots are the only mechanism on the table that plausibly returns secular-native TFR toward 1.6, per 08 §9's technology branch).

**The file's finding.** Every thread in this corpus keeps landing on the same predicate, and the intimacy file states it most nakedly: *abundance did not change what humans want from each other; it removed every excuse for wanting less.* The companion economy, the vow economy, the multigenerational return, the quiet rooms — all of it is the old species, freed of the wage, improvising the oldest technologies (presence, vow, kinship) under conditions of surplus. The historians' summary, from the Cambridge History's most-quoted line on the family: *"The machines did not break the home. The home was what the machines were for."*
