## THREAD 21 — Sovereign robot funds: the balance sheets

**The founding insight, priced.** The Transition's single largest wealth transfer ran through sovereign funds, because funds were the only institutions that could buy fleet equity *early* — before it was respectable, before it was legal to advertise, while the dividend politics were still riots. The window was 2036–2040: fleet consortia trading at 6–9× forward earnings amid Grid Riot headlines, politicians promising nationalization. The funds bought $2.3T of fleet equity in those five years at a blended 11× and held; by Y20 the same stakes were worth, on consensus, $9.1T. Norges Bank's **Botfund** extension (Y2) — the template — grew from $2.1T (canon, Y0) to **$5.6T by Y20** and $7.8T at Y30, paying each Norwegian ~$61,000/yr in implied distribution capacity against actual draws of $19,000 (the "handbrake rule": spend no more than 3% of trailing five-year NAV, after Alaska's lesson and before everyone else's).

**The allocations, Y30, across the eleven major funds** (Norges, Mubadala Compute, PIF-Verdant, Temasek GPR, the EU Citizen Fleet, the AFC futures funds, Kazakhstan's Samruk-Fleet, Chile's Cobre Fund, Botswana's Debswana-successor, Canada's Maple Fleet, Australia's Sunwire):

| Asset class | Weight | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Global fleet equity (ex-domestic) | 41% | The "index plus stewardship" sleeve; votes via the Fleet Protocol Foundation's public seats |
| Fleet infrastructure (energy, fabs, orbital) | 18% | The Sunbelt funds overweight here (Verdant 31%) |
| Sovereign bonds / Tap-paper | 16% | The robot-savings glut's landing pad |
| Materials and beneficiation | 9% | The AFC funds' specialty — the Lusaka Terms' industrial sequel |
| Care and healthspan | 7% | The "demography hedge" — everyone long aging, and paid |
| Craft/premium consumer | 4% | The Handmark belt |
| Cash and fleet-hour futures | 5% | Hedging the Idle Fleet's volatility (utilization cycles, Thread 1) |

**The governance fights that made the news.** The **Fund War (2044–47)**: Washington's discovery that foreign sovereign funds held 31% of OmniFleet and 24% of Kinetiq; the **Fleet-CFIUS statute (2046)** capped any single state at 9.9% of US-registered fleets, triggering the reallocation that made the *AFC futures funds* the unexpected winners — African funds bought what Gulf funds were forced to sell, which is why Lagos, not Abu Dhabi, is the largest foreign holder of American fleet equity at Y30 ($410B). The **dividend-vs-fund argument** (2046–48, the ADA's Title VII vs a Norwegian-style fund) was settled in the US by accretion rather than choice: the Fleet Participation Accounts *are* the American sovereign fund, owned by 258M people instead of the Treasury — the design 08 §11 called "the index settlement."

**The EU Citizen Fleet's mechanics** (full anatomy in Thread 24): one non-tradable share per EU resident at issuance, vesting to 40% tradable after ten years — by Y30 the Fund holds €3.9T and its per-capita account averages €7,200, thin by Norwegian standards and political gold by Polish ones.

**The AFC futures funds at Y30:** $610B combined AUM against the 2050s maturity schedule — Thread 36 takes the first dividend decade in detail.

**The verdict of the BIS's 2057 review** (*Owners of the Surplus*): sovereign funds captured roughly a fifth of the fleet economy's equity value on behalf of ~1.6B people, making them the era's quietest and most successful redistribution — and noting the irony that the funds' best purchases were made possible by exactly the political chaos they are now criticized for having declined to prevent.

## THREAD 22 — The insurance industry as silent governor: the pools in operation

**The three pools at Y30.** **TIAA-Prime** (US, the ex-TIAA/Chubb consolidation): $9.4T assets, 68% of North American robot-bond underwriting. **The Munich Stack** (EU: Munich Re-descendant core with the Euler Hermes-successor and six national pools): $8.1T. **The Lagos Mutual** (Afreximbank-capitalized, 2046+): $1.9T, growing 19%/yr — the Global South's pool, capitalized by the AFC futures funds' first maturities. The pools' combined premium income (~$780B/yr) exceeds the defense budgets of every country but the US and China — the fact that made *The Economist*'s 2049 cover ("THE INSURERS' PEACE") and gave the era its most-quoted institutional joke: *the world's fourth branch of government has a combined ratio.*

**Robot bonds, priced.** The bond is the premium instrument: capital posted against the harm a fleet can do, priced per class per thousand operating hours (Y30 schedule, illustrative): warehouse C-bot 6¢; delivery metal 11¢; agri-fleet 9¢; construction 14¢; crèche-class margaret 22¢ (the Margaret Rules' compliance discount: −30%); care-adjacent 31¢; human-contact-certified care 38¢ (the premium the sector's wages ride on); unattested autonomy — **no price, no license**: the pools simply will not quote, which is how the Seoul Regime and the Fleet Protocol enforce themselves without a policeman.

**The actuarial veto.** The pools' refusal to quote is the era's de facto rulemaking — the "silent governor" of 06 §4 — and its controversies are the governance file's richest: the **Insurance Strike of 2045** (all three pools refusing human-passing operators after LumenCare, collapsing the Skinwalker economy in nine months without a statute); the 2051 **ectogenesis refusal** (the pools declining unlicensed clinics, which the French Act had not anticipated — resolved by extending the licensed-cap regime); the 2054 **ghost-fleet premiums** (Calloway's 3% estimate priced in: the pools charge registered fleets a "ghost levy" — 2¢/1,000h — funding the inspectorates, socializing the dark fleet's externality). The 2052 **Insurance Compact** (the "Basel for pools") imposed capital, model-audit, and disclosure duties on the pools themselves, after the near-miss of 2051's MonoLine crisis (a care-reit bond collapse that spread through the Stack before the Compact's firewalls).

**The Hormuz cases — insurance as foreign policy.** The **2047 minelayer crisis** (08 §6): militia mining the strait against fleet transit; the pools declared the corridor a war zone, and the *repricing* (transit premiums 40×, then joint-pool escort contracts with two navies) ended the mining in eleven weeks — the TIAA-Prime/Munich joint pool's founding act. The **2053 incident** (cycle-2b register #12): a state escort standoff over fleet liens; the pools' joint declaration ("no coverage in either navy's engagement envelope") made the war zone economically uninhabitable for both, and the standoff settled at the table. The doctrine, stated in the Munich Stack's 2054 annual letter and since treated as law: *wherever the pools agree on a price, that price is the boundary of the possible.*

**The critiques, filed.** The plutocratic-drift reading (08 §11): the pools are unelected, their boards unrepresentative, their loss models proprietary — the CAOB fights of policing now recurring as "open the actuarial books" movements. The pools' answer — published-model summaries, the Insurance Compact's audit chambers — satisfies the auditors and not the theorists, and the corpus's honest note is that this argument has no Y30 resolution, only a standing ceasefire.

## THREAD 23 — Food systems and the agro-fleet revolution

**The productivity event.** Food was the transition's fastest-deflating major category: real food prices fell **−58% by Y15 and −71% by Y30** (the FAO real index), driven by stacked revolutions — the agro-fleets (precision everything; the Heartland Commons' "machines moving like weather"), green ammonia at $180/ton (2044, cycle-2a), the SDL pipeline's crop genetics (C4 rice at scale 2046; the 2049 drought-wheat releases), vertical farming's capture of the leafy-vegetable and strawberry categories (~30% of high-value produce in adapter nations by Y25), and precision fermentation proteins reaching **14% of global protein consumption by Y30** (the dairy herd down 40%, the beef herd down 28% — the methane line bending for reasons no climate treaty ever achieved).

**The labor event.** 2.1B people worked in agriculture at Y0 (26% of world labor; 60%+ in the poorest states). The fleet transition reached them *last* and *hardest*: dexterity tails in orchards and terraces ran a decade behind grain (parity Y2 in row crops, Y7 in orchards, Y11 in terraced rice); the displacement overlapped exactly with the Long Walk's rural push. The outcomes bifurcated per 08 §10: the **leapt** (Vietnam's coffee fleets, Brazil's agri-co-ops, the Commonwealth's farm co-ops, **Ethiopia's Adama Agro-Fleet** (2051) — 90,000 leased units running the Rift Valley's grain belt as a public utility, the era's largest single farming operation) and the **lagged** (the corridor states, where smallholders sold land to fleet co-ops at collapsed prices — the "second enclosure" the Luddite revival's historians always come back to).

**The land politics.** The Land Grab Decade's farmland chapter: Iowa $9k→$28k/acre (canon) was the global pattern in breadbaskets; the smallholder belts saw the opposite (fire sales). The settlements' answers: co-op acquisition rights (the Heartland model, exported via the AFC), the LVT's farm exemptions (and their decay, per 08 §7), and the EU's **"heirloom clause"** (2047: family farms holding 20+ years get fleet co-op membership by right). By Y30 the farm ownership map: co-ops 34% of operated acreage in adapter nations, sovereign/agri-funds 19%, family operators 41% (down from 78% but stabilized — the fleet made the family farm *viable* again by removing its labor problem and its debt service: the irony the rural historians savor is that the machine saved the family farm by ending the family's obligation to work it).

**The food culture.** Abundance's paradoxes: obesity's second wave (processed abundance) met the first generation of *cheap medical metabolism control* (the GLP-descendants' generic era, 2040s) and receded; the Handmark farm belt (certified-human agriculture: the Slow Guilds' markets, the "hand-harvest" premium wines and teas) is a $90B niche that behaves exactly like organic's ancestor; the **"harvest footage"** economy (05's stock image) matured into a genre — the agri-co-ops employ more certified-human documentarians than agronomists, because the film of the last human harvest is the century's recurring elegy.

**The risk file.** The Ulaanbaatar grain shock (2042) and the 2049 **Coffee Rust Emergency** (the fleet-managed replanting of Central America in fourteen months — the first natural disaster beaten primarily by fleet logistics) rewrote the food-security playbook: *famines are logistics failures, not harvest failures* (cycle-2b register #7), and by Y30 the WFP-fleet standby is the era's most-liked institution (74% global approval, the surveys say, for what is functionally a very large inventory of robots and grain).

## THREAD 24 — The EU settlement, anatomized: Citizen Fleet, co-determination, the Statut Universel

**The Brussels Compromise (2040) — the four pillars in full.** (1) The **€900/month presence floor** (nationally topped-up, EU-cofinanced at 30% through the Structural Presence Fund). (2) **Fleet co-determination**: 15% of supervisory seats of every fleet operator above 5,000 units operating in the Union — the Rhine tradition applied to the machines, with the seats elected by a *resident* electorate, not employees (the "everyone is an employee" doctrine, the Compromise's most-quoted line). (3) The **Citizen Fleet**: the EU-level fund buying fleet equity from the structural-cohesion budget — one non-tradable share per resident, vesting 40% tradable at ten years; €3.9T by Y30, average per-capita account €7,200 (€2,100 in Bulgaria, €14,000 in Luxembourg — the convergence machine inside the divergence complaint). (4) The **human-premium certification regime** (the Handmark's legal home; the care-directive's "noticing" requirement).

**The Sequel Acts.** The **Statut Universel** (France, 2042): the pension system converted to a universal presence payment — retirement dies as a category five years before the rest of the world notices it has; Germany's **Fleet Directive** (2049): the Fleet Protocol adopted by regulation (the protocolization's fastest export); the **Electron Dividend** (2051): EU residents receive a small per-capita share of Grid Union throughput revenue — the MWh levy's citizen face, €11–14/month and the EU's most popular program by 2054.

**The machinery and the coma.** The Union's speed problem was solved the only way Brussels solves anything: by building a fast lane. The **Enhanced Cooperation fleet framework** (2050) let twelve-or-more states proceed without unanimity — the "consensus coma" of 2049–52 (fourteen parliaments, one directive) ended when the coma's own statistics (EU GDP/cap growing 0.9 points slower than the fast lane's) made unanimity embarrassing. Poland's rise (the robot-manufacturing hub, $14/hr legacy workforce canon, Wrocław and Poznań's fleet-plant belt) is the Union's self-reconciling argument: the slow bloc's cohesion bought the fastest *internal* convergence in its history.

**The failure handled, the failure deferred.** Hungary (Orban Template, 2043): suspended from co-determination councils within a year, quota-frozen, and — the mechanism the corpus emphasizes — *fenced by the Citizen Fleet*: Budapest could tier its loyalty dividends, but the Fleet's shares are registered per-capita and non-forfeitable, so the clientelism could tax the floor but never own the citizen. (Hungary's Y30 status: Template regime, still standing, demographics and the fleet's quiet attrition against it — the EU's unresolved wound.) Deferred: the Ukraine recovery fleet-mandate (the 2046–52 reconstruction ran as the Union's largest fleet deployment, 380,000 units, and its governance questions — who owns the rebuilt grid's fleets — remain the accession file's hardest pages).

**The EU at Y30.** The adapter bloc with the flattest distribution (consumption Gini 0.19), the slowest growth (GWO-share down 2 points), the deepest legitimacy (turnout 76%), and the strongest export: its *law*. The Fleet Directive, the care directives, the Statut Universel model, and the co-determination doctrine are the union of twenty-seven's real product — the "Brussels effect," reborn for the fleet century and, per the comparative scholars, the reason the Orban Template stalled in Europe after two tries while running nine-for-fourteen elsewhere.

## THREAD 25 — The Long Walk: a dedicated history

*The corpus's moral center, until now distributed across files. The Walkers deserve their own thread.*

**The anatomy.** 2038–2046; **~90M displaced** in the South Asian interior; **~1.9M excess deaths** (the actuarial reconstruction of 2052: cholera and waterborne disease 41%, heat 27%, violence 14%, childbirth and treatable illness 12%, the remainder unclassified). The corridors: the Bengal delta outward (the garment collapse's 3.1M jobs dying in eighteen months plus salinity taking the fields); the Deccan's dead service towns (the Service Export Cliff's interior echo); the Indus basin's heat belt. The displaced are the **Walkers** (canon slang) — the name from the news photos of the 2040 Grand Trunk Road columns, and adopted, over the objection of everyone who wasn't there, by everyone who was.

**The response, honestly graded.** India's Right to Contribution Act (2040, ~90M enrolled — the numerical coincidence historians note with discomfort) functioned in its first three years as a census of the desperate rather than a program: 44% muster absenteeism, the ghost-muster economy, the camps' private economies. The 2044 redesign (panchayat administration, work menus, the contribution wage re-indexed) made it functional; the **National Robotics Mission's work camps** (water works, canal rebuilding, the Eastern Dedicated Freight corridor's completion) gave the Walkers' generation its first claim on the state. Pakistan's collapse removed the western corridor's already-thin safety net (canon failure list); Bangladesh's Delta Emergency (2041) was the humanitarian low-water mark — the camps at Cox's Bazar tripling, the fleet-solar purification barges arriving years late and then never leaving.

**The accounting fight.** The 1.9M figure is the *Delhi Compact's* designated controversy: the Indian government's 2048 count said 1.2M; the People's Archive's community enumeration said 2.6M; the 2052 actuarial compromise — 1.9M, ±0.3 — is taught with its provenance, per the Attested Record standard's hardest test. The memorial arithmetic is settled differently: the **Badagry Road precedent** (07) — memorials co-designed by the displaced — became the template for the **Corridor Memorials** (2056+): at the Grand Trunk's mile-markers, the names where names exist, the counts where they don't.

**The Walkers at Y30.** The cohort in their twenties and thirties dominates South Asian letters (the "corridor memoirs" are the subcontinent's dominant genre), state politics (the Contribution Cohorts' electoral wave from 2051 — Thread 37 takes the 2050s), and the demography of the interior second cities (Nagpur, Raipur, Ludhiana: grew 30–45% Y10–Y25 on Walker settlement). The Trust Divide finding (05 §1) has its harshest case here: fertility among settled Walker households recovered to 1.9 by Y30 — *above* the Indian mean — the mutual-aid camps' reconstituted-village structure doing exactly what the high-trust model predicts, in the worst circumstance the model was ever asked to fit.

**What the sim owes this thread.** The Transition's settlement story — dividends, protocols, co-determination — is an OECD story, and the Long Walk is the corpus's reminder of what the same price-crossing did where the fiscal inversion never arrived. The *Cambridge History*'s verdict on the decade, which the editorial board refused to soften: *"The fleet century's abundance was real, and its arrival in South Asia was late by any measure that matters. The Walkers did not wait for it. They built the villages it would eventually reach."*
